# 94. Live map — the game's own sprites and animations over the artwork

**Status:** open — shipped 2026-10-02 as the sprites at their spawn frame, the loops at header timing, the player-free cycles and the creatures' brains and patrols under the no-player reading, with the game's player-free effects; the Markers-for-the-rest toggle 2026-10-03; the objects as themselves and the patrols as the defaults 2026-10-04; open: a Fleech's hoist climb, the fall at a line's end, the Elum, the LCD text, the per-layer foreground masks and the drop shadows · **Effort:** the open slices small to medium (a brain each for the walkers' gaps; a disc rebuild of the masks; a shadow rule per creature) · **Where:** the mask split needs a disc; the rest anywhere · **Filed:** 2026-09-17 investigation over the pinned decomp, upstream `beta` and paulsapps/alive

## What and why

A Display toggle that makes the map come alive: every placed object drawn as the sprite the game draws for it instead of a rectangle, playing the animation the game plays — mines flashing, UXBs blinking their pattern, mudokons scrubbing and chiselling, sligs at their posts, doves circling a portal — with nothing hand-made. Sprites, frame timing and motion are to come from the PS1 discs and the alive_reversing decompilation alone, so a rebuild reproduces them byte for byte, which is the bar every other layer of this map already meets.

**Outcome of the investigation: the sprites and their animation timing are data on the discs and meet that bar; motion is game code that runs relative to the player, and meets it under one reading, that there is no player in the world.** Everything below is the evidence for the two halves of that sentence and what each half costs; the reading itself is stated under *Shipped*.

## What was checked (2026-09-17)

Three trees were read: the decomp at the revision the builder pins (`e2badb8df`, master lineage), upstream AliveTeam `beta` at `464ad4a61` (2026-09-06, the "relive" rewrite: `Source/relive_lib/**`, a data-conversion stage, game objects renamed with `AnimId` enumerators; the fork this repo sits beside had a `beta` 141 commits behind it), and paulsapps/alive, the 2015–2020 community engine that read both the PC and the PS1 data. Nothing here has been measured on a disc yet, and every claim that needs one is listed under *The disc probe*.

### Where the pixels are, and what reads them

**The sprites live only on the discs, in the BAN and BND files of each LVL archive and in a few CAM files, as `Anim` chunks of the same 16-byte chunk container `parse_chunks` already walks for the cameras.** relive ships no artwork; its data conversion reads the user's own PC files. So the disc is required for this feature the way it is for the camera art, and for nothing less: the decomp holds the format and the rules, never a pixel.

**The format is fully specified by the pinned decomp and restated in `beta`'s converter, and the PS1 files carry the same layouts.** An `Anim` resource opens with `{s16 max_w, s16 max_h, u32 frame_table_offset, u32 word}`; each frame is a 12-byte `FrameHeader` `{u32 clut_offset, u8 w, u8 h, u8 bpp, u8 compression, u16, u16}` followed by its payload; each frame's placement is a 20-byte `FrameInfoHeader` `{u32 frame_header_offset, s16 magic, s16 count, s16 x_off, y_off, min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y}` plus `count` callback points; and each animation is an `AnimationHeader` `{u16 fps, s16 num_frames, s16 loop_start, u16 flags, u32 frame_offsets[n]}`, flags `1` never-unload, `2` loop, `4`/`8` flip that no consumer reads. Every offset is from byte 0 of the chunk's data. The CLUT is `u32 count` then RGB555 words, bit 15 the semi-transparency bit; pixels are 4- or 8-bit indices, the low nibble the left pixel, and 16-bit frames occur in none of the PC data (`beta`'s converter aborts on one; the probe asks the discs). alive parses PC and PS1 files through one code path and forks only at the frame decoder (`src/oddlib/anim.cpp`), and the decomp's own flag values were "reversed by editing data in memory on PSX version" (`Source/AliveLibAE/Animation.hpp`), so the header layouts are not a platform question. What `beta` adds over the pin is a working PNG+JSON converter to port from (`Source/relive_lib/data_conversion/AnimationConverter.cpp`), the `AnimId` names, and a corrected record table; the format knowledge itself is the same in both.

**Frame timing is in the header, and the rule is small.** `fps` is a hold count in engine ticks, typically 1 or 2: at construction the animation stands at frame 0 with its counter at 1, the first tick decodes frame 0 again and reloads the counter from `fps`, and every frame after that is held `fps` ticks; on running past the last frame a looping animation returns to `loop_start` and a non-looping one parks with its counter at 0 (`Animation.cpp` in either game, `AnimationBase.cpp` in `beta`). The tick is the main-loop pass, paced to 30 Hz by a two-vblank wait inside the render (`PsxDisplay.cpp`), which is the `LOGIC_FPS` the map already counts by; two vblanks would be 25 Hz on a PAL disc, and the discs here are SLUS, so 30 stands. Almost nothing overwrites the delay (a particle and the mine car do); an object that needs another cadence calls `SetFrame` or stops animating, so "loop at header timing" is exactly what the engine does for an animation left alone.

**The frame decoders are where the platforms part, and Exoddus needs one the decomp does not have.** Type 0 is raw; type 2 is a 3-to-4-byte unpack; types 4 and 5 are the LZ the builder already ports for FG1 (`decompress_4or5`); type 3 is two different algorithms under one tag, the Oddysee one in `Source/AliveLibAO/Compression.cpp` and the Exoddus one in `Source/AliveLibAE/Compression.cpp`; PC Exoddus adds a nibble RLE as type 6. Oddysee on PS1 needs nothing beyond those: alive consults its PS1 flag only in cases 6 and 7 and notes "AO cases end at 5". Exoddus on PS1 tags frames 6 and 7, and those are a different algorithm — a bit-buffer code-table scheme — that alive implements as `CompressionType6or7AePsx` in 8- and 6-bit variants, reversed from a function in the AE PSX **demo**, introduced in a commit saying it "has a few bugs", and applied after clipping one row off the frame height "to remove extra bad pixels". Both decomp trees abort on ids 7 and 8 and would read a PS1 type-6 frame with the PC algorithm, and `beta`'s converter has no PS1 input path of any kind (its cam stitcher classifies every PS1 cam as Exoddus PC and its FG1 reader aborts on the compressed sub-stream both PS1 games use, which is the `CamConverter` behaviour already recorded in [docs/format-gotchas.md](../docs/format-gotchas.md)). alive is MIT, so its decoder can be ported; whether it is byte-exact against the retail discs is the first thing the probe has to establish, ideally against an emulator's VRAM.

**Which animation is which cannot be copied from the PC tables.** `beta`'s `kAnimRecords` (`Source/relive_lib/AnimResources.cpp`) names, per `AnimId`, the BAN or BND file, the resource id, the maximum frame size and the **byte offset of the animation's header inside the chunk**, in an Exoddus column and an Oddysee column — 1,025 ids plus `None`, 116 of them background animations. Those offsets are the PC files' and already differ between the two PC games for the same sprite (`Mine` 784 against 748, `Slig_Idle` 135512 against 132740), and the converter carries a per-file fix-up for one Exoddus BND whose recorded offsets do not match the file it meets. The frame pixels precede the header tables, and PS1 frames compress differently, so no PS1 offset can be expected to land. What does transfer is the key alive used: **file name, resource id, and the animation's ordinal in the chunk's table**. Over alive's inventory of real discs, every one of the 313 Exoddus `(file, id)` pairs it saw on both PC and CD1 carries the same animation count on both, and 669 of 674 Oddysee pairs do — the five exceptions are one blood chunk (three PC animations, one PS1) repeated in four Lines cameras. Exoddus's 377 PC pairs are exactly the union of CD1's 313 and CD2's 349, so the builder's both-discs rule covers sprites too. The headers pack contiguously in the recorded data (every consecutive pair of recorded offsets differs by `8 + 4k` but one), so a chunk's animations can be enumerated by walking from the file header's `frame_table_offset` to the chunk end with each header validated — the enumeration is what makes the ordinal key usable without trusting a PC offset.

**Two PS1 particulars the decomp has no path for.** alive documents an Exoddus PS1 variant where the header word at +8 is non-zero and the resource is one pre-made sprite sheet whose frame infos are sub-rectangles (detected by that word plus ten zero bytes after it), with no list of which files exhibit it; the pinned decomp aborts if that word is non-zero and `beta` calls it the Oddysee CLUT size. And PC Exoddus stores frame x-offsets in the PC's 640-wide space (alive divides them by 640/368 for its PC datasets only, and the decomp's `PsxToPCX` exists for that framebuffer), so PS1 data should be native to the 368-wide window the map draws in — alive's working assumption, which the probe confirms by overlaying a decoded frame on a committed camera at its object's anchor.

### Which animation an object shows at spawn is code

**One `AnimId` per class, chosen by the constructor from TLV fields and the level, and the state it stands in includes the brain's first tick.** Read over every placed type in both games (`Source/AliveLibAE`, `Source/AliveLibAO`, `Source/relive_lib/GameObjects` and `Factory.cpp` in `beta`, the pin where `beta` simplified): a Slig is `Slig_Idle` or, with `start_state` sleeping, `Slig_Sleeping` from a second BAN; an Oddysee Mudokon's `job` picks stand-scrub, sit-scrub or sit-chant and the constructor ends in a `VUpdate`, so it is already in `StandScrubLoop` rather than idle; an Exoddus Mudokon's `state` picks chisel, scrub, angry worker or ring giver and `emotion` overrides two of those; a UXB is `UXB_Active` or, off, a static `UXB_Disabled`; a Mine is its loop with a **static** flash overlay (the overlay is drawn and never updated); doors, levers, lifts, trapdoors and ropes take level-themed files. Counted over the shipped data: Oddysee places 5,625 objects of which 2,331 belong to the 48 types that draw a sprite, 137 to types that draw font text or vector particles (the LCDs, the gas countdown, the hint fly, the Zs over a slog hut), and 3,157 to 41 invisible marker types; Exoddus places 10,602, of which 4,702 in 52 sprite types, 334 text or procedural, 5,566 invisible across 40 types. Oddysee's menu level `S1` holds five menu controllers and three preloaders and no sprite at all.

**Position is a rule per type, and the rules reach for data the map already ships — plus one it cannot.** The common shape is the rect's mid x and a raycast from the rect's top against the collision lines (floor, or walls-or-floor in Oddysee; the foreground or background set by the scale word), a rule the builder can evaluate over `lines`. Then the exceptions: every working Mudokon and every Slig has its x **snapped to the engine's grid** (25 units at full scale, 13 at half) after the read, in Exoddus always and in Oddysee only when the object lies inside the active camera's 1,024-wide span — so an Oddysee mud's resting x can depend on which camera was active when it spawned, which no disc reproduces; ring and lift muds shift by 8; a Glukkon raycasts 79 down; a portal stands 55 above its floor hit; a slig or mine that finds no floor is falling, not idle. Half scale is a sprite scale of 0.5 and a different render layer; facing is `start_direction` for most creatures, none for a Scrab; and a frame is placed at the object's position plus the frame's own `(x_off, y_off)` scaled, with per-game rounding differences the render formula in `Animation.cpp` spells out (Exoddus adds a pixel to a half-scale frame's size; Oddysee rounds a flipped x once). The map draws in world units, which is what an object's position is, so the engine's camera arithmetic (Oddysee's camera point being the window's centre, 184/120 from its corner) never enters the placement.

**Colour is not the palette alone.** The frame's own CLUT is the default; a few overrides are separate `Palt` chunks applied by code, and two of them are TLV data the archive already holds — an Exoddus Mudokon's `blind` picks the blind palette, an Oddysee background Glukkon's `pal_id` (825–828) picks one of four palettes from `GLUKKON.BND` — while UXB's green flash paints a second, overlaid animation and the Stock Yards slig palette is one level's. On top of that nearly every Exoddus creature and prop is **tinted per type and per level** through `SetTint` tables in code (a Mudokon is 87/103/67 where neutral is 128, a Slig 102/127/118, a Scrab 137 in Necrum and 127 elsewhere; Oddysee has fewer, the Stock Yards ones the largest), shadow zones add a positional term at render time, and every physics object's polygon is semi-transparent with the blend mode 50/50 by default, so a CLUT entry with bit 15 set blends with the camera behind it while one with the bit clear is opaque and index 0x0000 is skipped. A raw-CLUT render is therefore wrong for most Exoddus creatures; the tint tables are code the rule port has to carry, and the atlas has to keep three texel states (clear, opaque, blend).

**Draw order is a layer per animation, not a scale.** Every half-scale object takes a `_Half_` layer that sits below every full-scale layer, background animations take theirs from their own TLV field, and within a layer later-constructed objects draw beneath earlier ones. The FG1 foreground blocks the map ships as one flattened mask are in the game **four layers** (`Source/AliveLibAE/FG1.cpp` maps a block's layer id 0–3 to well-half, FG1-half, well, FG1), two of which sit below every sprite and two above every ordinary sprite: `decode_fg1` unpacks the block's layer and drops it. Correct occlusion therefore needs the fg PNGs split per block layer, which is a disc rebuild of every fg mask, and the viewer's fg pass today is a diagnostic drawn tinted at 0.6 alpha over everything rather than a compositing layer — a sprite under it would be tinted through, a sprite over it would invert the game.

**One TLV can be several draws, and some draws have no TLV.** A LiftPoint draws its platform, a level-indexed wheel at the Pulley's position and two ropes it constructs itself; twenty Exoddus creature classes construct a Shadow drawn at the floor line; a chanting mud emits a tint particle every eight ticks; a MotionDetector owns its laser; the slig bound markers spawn the slig whose persist id matches, in cameras two or three away. A sidecar of one `{atlas, animation, frame}` per object cannot express this: it needs a list of draw records per object, including the ones the game spawns beside it.

**What is visible at spawn depends on switch state, and the level-start state is not the only one the code computes.** Electric walls render when the switch differs from `start_state`; door flames, flintlock fire, moving bombs' start and status lights read switches in the constructor; security doors and the Glukkon help phones spawn with rendering off until the player is near; sling muds appear twelve ticks after Abe enters their camera; an Exoddus portal exists at all only if `create_portal_switch_id` reads on (129 of 135 shipped carry id 1, which the engine hardwires to on). Ids 2–255 are zeroed on a plain level transition and on a new game, so "all off" is a defensible convention — except that FeeCo's doors read save-progress flags in their constructor (a visited Bonewerkz closes one) and fire that logic under the base level id but not under its ender id, and the lift and door art tables are indexed by level id with the ender ids as their own rows. The map lists one level for a base and its ender, so the sidecar has to pick one id per level and say so.

**Level-themed art is a code switch, and Barracks doors need a column the builder drops.** In Exoddus the door factory switches on the level id to load one of six door BANs, every one under the same resource id, and picks `SHDOOR.BAN` in Barracks when the path's overlay id is 108 — the low byte of the `field_C_overlay_id` column of the PC path table that `parse_pathdata_cpp` does not capture; Oddysee's per-level door BND carries three resource ids chosen by the door's `start_state` word (door, hub door, final test door), and five level rows have no door art at all, the object dying. Lifts follow the same pattern over eight Exoddus and five Oddysee BNDs, trapdoors and ropes over vault and non-vault variants, Oddysee levers over three files; Exoddus's lever and Oddysee's foot switch are one file everywhere. `beta` collapses this into a per-door theme string computed at conversion from level, overlay and door kind (`relive_tlvs_conversion.hpp`), which is a good cross-check for the table the builder would hold.

**Background animations are already in the builder's hands.** A BackgroundAnimation's `animation_id` is the resource id of an `Anim` chunk inside the camera's own CAM file (one exception, `MINEFAN.BAN`), the same chunks `decode_cam` takes `Bits` from; Oddysee's 57 placements collapse to 27 ids and Exoddus's 68 to 5, and every one has a row in `beta`'s table. Oddysee's wells do the same: 85 of its 89 carry a non-zero `animation_id` naming a CAM-resident animation, where Exoddus's 266 all carry 0 and draw leaves only. The LCD boards, the gas countdown and the colourful meter are `LCDFONT.FNT` glyphs, not sprites, and stay out.

### Motion is not data

**The engine is deterministic and could be recorded, but what it would record is a play, not an object's idle.** Every gameplay quantity is s32 fixed point; the random generator is a 256-byte table walked by a global byte index that nothing seeds from a clock, with a handful of classes keeping private indices into the same table (leaves at 8, the hint fly at 37, blood from the frame counter); and the team's own record-and-replay harness validates every alive object's position and motion per frame, which is a determinism proof. But three facts stand in the way of "as in the game" for a map. **Only the current camera's TLVs are ever constructed** — the four orthogonal neighbours load resources only (`Map.cpp` in either game), so a whole path is never live at once and an object's behaviour depends on which camera the player stands in. **Idle behaviour is player-relative by construction**: a Slog, a Paramite and a MovingBomb dereference the controlled character with no null check, a Slog and a Paramite stop animating beyond 750×390 units from Abe and a Mudokon beyond 750×520, a UXB and a MovingBomb destroy themselves beyond 750×520, and a Slig's idle brain reads Abe's facing, visibility and height every tick before deciding to patrol (`Slig.cpp`, `Slog.cpp`, `Mudokon.cpp`, `UXB.cpp` in `Source/AliveLibAE`). **And every timed idle draws from the shared random stream**: a working mud alternates work and pause on 35 + rnd%64 tick timers and turns every 240 + rnd%64; a slog scratches on 120 + rnd%32 and growls on 60 + rnd%32, with both timers unseeded on an awake spawn so they fire at once; drills and meat saws pause for a random range between strokes; a paramite turns on a random roll. The stream is one sequence shared by every object in update order, so an object's phase is not a property of the object and no fixed seed recovers it. One more thing is not on the disc at all: an Oddysee slig placed without bound markers patrols between whatever uninitialised memory holds, and the 12809/6405 span relive gives it is relive's own fix.

**The three routes to faithful motion each looked blocked.** An offline trace from a native relive build would need PC LVL files, which this project does not have and relive cannot be pointed at PS1 data for (above), plus a player object per camera that the engine's culling then measures everything against, plus the default build's own `ORIGINAL_GAME_FIXES`; the result would have to be labelled a recording of one Abe placement. Running relive in the page over WebAssembly adds a port with zero Emscripten references, SDL3, an OpenGL 3 renderer, AV1 video, a thread pool and forced sanitizers, over the same PC-data dependency. Hand-porting the creatures' state machines to JavaScript means 3,000–7,000 lines per creature (Exoddus Slig 6,630 lines with 82 motion functions, Mudokon 7,154, Paramite 6,211, Fleech 4,245, Scrab 4,232, Slog 3,411) whose idle is defined relative to a player that would have to be simulated too. Read with no player in the world, that third route is the one that shipped: the player-relative branches resolve one way, and a creature's idle and patrol brain comes to a few hundred lines rather than thousands.

**What is player-free and random-free is small, and it is the part worth porting.** A UXB's blink is a state machine over its `pattern` and `pattern_length`: a two-tick flash, then red ticks at a ten-tick delay, each digit being the count of red ticks before one green, a twelve-tick period with no random draw and no player read outside its distance cull. A MotionDetector's laser spawns at the left or right edge of its rect by `initial_move_direction` and sweeps between the edges at `speed / 256` units per tick with a fifteen-tick pause at each end, reading the player only to raise the alarm. A StatusLight blinks four ticks on, four off, by its switch. A worker or shrykull portal's six doves orbit at four angle units per tick, sixty-four ticks a revolution, on a 30×35 ellipse scaled — an Abe portal's doves instead pulse their x radius off a static the whole level shares. An electric wall flips every eight ticks from a random start frame. Everything else that moves — sligs walking their bounds, scrabs, slurgs, greeters at three units per tick, flying sligs on their track lines, moving bombs along theirs (started by a switch), drills and saws, bats, bees — is either switch-started, random-paced or player-gated, and would be shown at its spawn frame with its loop.

### Where it plugs into the map

**Builder.** A new stage beside `decode_cam` reads each level's BAN/BND files through `Lvl.read` and `parse_chunks`, enumerates each `Anim` chunk's animations, decodes the frames it needs and packs them into per-level atlas PNGs through `write_png`, so oxipng pins the bytes the way it pins the cameras; the packing must iterate sorted keys and nothing hash-ordered. Placed under `cams/<game>/<LEVEL>/` an atlas inherits the worker's cache-first rule, the skip-if-exists reuse and the `--levels` subset merge for free — and re-stamps `CACHE_NAME`, which evicts every visitor's cached artwork for both games on any atlas change, and stays out of the offline download, whose file list is `camFiles` (png and fg only) and whose "complete" claim would then be false. Every atlas is a disc-machine artefact like a camera; the per-object sidecar (which animation, which frame, position, scale, flip, layer, tint, palette) can be built anywhere from the decomp's rules and the committed `map_data`, and belongs in a `sprites_{ao,ae}.json` fetched when the toggle turns on, the `relive_export` precedent, never in `fields` (the raw archive, byte-identical by contract) and never duplicating an `extra` key.

**Viewer.** The sprite pass sits inside `paintMarkers`' TLV loop and obeys its four rules: `markerShown`, framing on `drawBox`, the `screenRuns` clip so a sprite folded into the packed layout's slack does not paint over a neighbouring screen (a frame can extend past its TLV rect and past the window; the PS1 GPU clips it to the draw area and the map has to), and `onBackgroundPlane`, which is a label-resolved rule and not `scale === 1` (InvisibleSwitch and GlukkonSwitch invert it, DoorFlame's 2 is half), so the half-scale decision must go through the label. Sprites draw 1:1 like the artwork with smoothing only when zoomed out. A Display toggle is pinned in seven places (the sidebar map, `SHOW_KEYS`, the `SHOW_KEY` letter map, the world graph's `MAP_KEYS` swallow set, the `index.html` row, the shortcuts dialog, `QUIET_SHOW` in the browser helpers), only the sidebar–`SHOW_KEYS` parity being test-enforced; the free letters are b d h j k n o q t u x y z. The loop is the viewer's first steady-state animation loop besides the flash that [47](item-047-held-flash-repaints.md) had to cap: it runs only while the toggle is on, the tab visible and the world graph down, ticks the logic clock at `LOGIC_FPS` rather than at the display rate, and reads `prefers-reduced-motion` through `matchMedia` (the stylesheet's rule cannot reach a canvas). An export draws a fixed frame — the spawn frame — so two exports stay byte-identical, and the atlases must join `preloadPath`, `artworkReady` and the image cache's keep set or an export paints blanks and a browse past 500 images drops them. Hover and the ruler keep the TLV rect. `anchors.spec` keeps its pixel pins with the toggle off; a sprite on would only trip the artwork check outside the 32-pixel pad around each marker box, so the feature needs pins of its own: atlas byte-reproducibility per level, every sprite type resolving a draw record, the render formula on a few placements against the artwork.

**Edited objects.** The level editor sandbox ([42](item-042-level-editor.md)) changes exactly the fields the spawn rules read — scale, `start_state`, direction, `state`, `emotion`, switch ids — so a build-time sidecar goes stale under an edit. Either the per-type spawn rules are ported to JavaScript as `extra.js` ported the navigation rules, a second port of the same fifty types, or an edited object draws its pristine sprite and is marked, under the "edited state is marked wherever it shows" rule.

**Size and rights.** The committed artwork is 2,888 PNGs at 205 MB and the pack about 188 MiB, ten megabytes past the figure [73](item-073-repo-size-trajectory.md) closed on as the floor on the premise that no further artwork was planned; every atlas is permanent pack weight and a re-emit doubles it, so a byte budget has to be named before the first atlas is committed, and the probe measures what a level's sprites come to. Sprite sheets are extracted imagery, the class the cameras are in and the README already words ("the extracted imagery remains the property of its copyright holders"), not the audio class [22](item-022-level-path-music.md) deferred; but they are a new kind of that class, and the About dialog and README have to name it, which touches [68](item-068-repo-licensing.md). alive's decoders are MIT; alive_reversing ships no licence file (its README carries a contributor-consent clause), which is the footing the builder's existing parsing of its headers already stands on.

**The `beta` branch.** Worth reading and not worth pinning. It is where the per-`AnimId` file/resource-id table with both games' columns, the per-level conversion table with the door themes, the named palette records and the cleaned-up game objects live, and its converter is the port template; but it is a PC-only pipeline, its TLV definitions moved out of the `relive_api` headers `schema.py` parses (no `CTOR_AO`/`CTOR_AE` block exists under `beta`'s `Source/`), so the builder's caches cannot be re-pinned to it, and its offsets are PC offsets. The right use is a second foreign pin for a curated table this repo owns — `AnimId` → (file, resource id, ordinal) per game — derived from `beta`'s columns and **validated against the disc**, the way `annotations.json` is curated against the shipped data.

## What the discs said (2026-10-02)

The probe ran against `~/workplace/discs` through the builder's own `Disc`, `Lvl` and `parse_chunks`, and every format question above closed:

- **The layouts are the decomp's.** Every `Anim` chunk on both games walks clean: 2,985 Oddysee chunks and 1,700 Exoddus ones, 98,829 frame references decoded without an error. The header names the *last* animation table, not the first; the tables pack contiguously after the final frame-info record, so the walk steps back from the named table's own frame infos and runs forward until it passes through it. A zero-frame table (AO `ROPES.BAN`) keeps its slot, and the one real table before it is found by widening the search. Checked against alive's inventory of the same discs, the walk agrees on 667 of 670 Oddysee chunks and 375 of 377 Exoddus ones, the rest being zero-frame tables alive drops and the walk counts.
- **Oddysee needs nothing beyond the decomp; Exoddus needs alive's decoder.** PS1 Oddysee frames use compression ids 0, 2, 3 and 4 only. PS1 Exoddus tags 10,392 of its 11,045 frames 6 or 7, the pair-substitution scheme only alive implements. Ported to Python it decodes every frame, and alive's one-row clip turned out to be alive's own stride bug: the port never produces more bytes than the frame holds, the output simply stops where the input ends and the rest of the frame stays zero. 260 Exoddus chunks are the pre-rendered-sheet shape, their frame infos carrying `x, y, w, h` sub-rectangles in the header-offset slot.
- **The PC offsets do solve to ordinals.** The table region's layout is the same sequence of header sizes on both platforms, so the recorded PC offsets of a chunk's several records pin the alignment: 482 chunks resolve to a unique ordinal, 11 to two candidates (the lift wheels, whose one uniquely solvable chunk, `RLIFT.BND`, shows both records to be the turning animations of two wheel designs, which settles the rest; `ABEDOOR`, `ABENOELM`, `DOGBLOW`, `R1ROPES`), 3 chunks are absent (`PARAMITE.BND` 611, `ROLLROCK.BAN`, `STAR.BAN`, none drawn at spawn).
- **One copy per asset.** No `(file, resource id)` chunk differs across levels in either game, none differs between the two Exoddus discs, and a same-id camera animation is identical in every camera carrying it. The per-level lift and door files that share a resource id are byte-identical where the decomp's tables treat them alike (`FDLIFT`/`BALIFT`/`BWLIFT`/`BRLIFT` are `MILIFT`, `PVLIFT`/`SVLIFT` are `NELIFT`) and differ where they name a theme of their own; `NECROPE.BAN` differs from `ROPES.BAN`, so the Necrum rope is an entry of its own.
- **Every palette and background animation is where the code says.** Every `Palt` chunk `beta`'s palette records name is present (the bomb's sits in `E1BOMB.BAN`, not `BOMB.BND`), and all 111 Oddysee and 5 Exoddus placed background-animation ids, Oddysee's 85 well animations among them, resolve to a camera-resident chunk.
- **The semi-transparency bit is per object.** About a quarter of an ordinary sprite's pixels sit on CLUT entries carrying bit 15 (7 of 64 entries, the soft edges), while a Dove's whole palette does and the Dove clears the polygon flag; the Mudokon sets it. So the atlas keeps the bit as a third alpha state and the viewer blends it only for objects whose constructor leaves the flag on.
- **FG1 layers.** Oddysee's masks use layer ids 0 and 1, both above its sprites; Exoddus uses all four, and the two that sit under sprites ("well") appear in 239 and 63 of its 1,155 cameras. The flattened masks the map ships draw over sprites everywhere, which is right for Oddysee and wrong under those Exoddus blocks: the per-layer split is the open slice.

## The disc probe

A read-only pass over the images, before any decoder is ported; the answers decide the plan and several of them can close it. Each is a few lines over `Disc`, `Lvl` and `parse_chunks`.

1. Per level (largest copy, both Exoddus discs), list every `Anim` chunk by file and resource id, and check each `(file, id)` `beta`'s `kAnimRecords` names for a placed type exists; list the resource ids of the `Palt` chunks (`MUDPAL.BND`, `UXB.BND`'s green flash, `GLUKKON.BND` 825–828, `E1PAL.BAN`, the Stock Yards `TBMBPAL.BAN`).
2. Histogram every frame's compression type and bit depth per game: Oddysee within 0–5, Exoddus using 6 and 7, no type 1, no 16-bit; count Exoddus chunks whose +8 word is non-zero followed by ten zero bytes (alive's sprite-sheet form) and name the files.
3. Walk each chunk's animation headers from `frame_table_offset` to the chunk end with validation, compare the count per `(file, id)` against alive's per-dataset counts, and test whether any PC offset from `beta`'s table lands on a valid header (expected: almost none).
4. Decode one frame per codec with the decomp's algorithms and, for Exoddus 6/7, a port of alive's, with and without its one-row clip; render a UXB, a Mine and a Slig at their spawn positions over the committed camera PNG to settle the anchor, the scale and the 368-space question.
5. Hash same-named files across levels (`SLIG.BND` in MI, BA, BR; `DOOR.BAN` where it recurs) and across the two Exoddus discs: whether a name ever carries different content per level decides whether one entry per asset suffices outside the themed set.
6. Confirm the CAM-resident animations: every placed `animation_id` (Oddysee 27 ids and the 85 well ids, Exoddus 5 and `MINEFAN.BAN`) resolves to an `Anim` chunk in its camera's file, and read their frame counts and `fps`.
7. Histogram the FG1 block layer ids per game, which sizes the per-layer fg split and says whether Oddysee uses more than the two layers the decomp's native table names.
8. Measure: the sprites one level would need, decoded and packed through `write_png`, in bytes per game, against the budget question above.

## Watch out

- The Oddysee grid snap is camera-relative: a working mud's x depends on the active camera at spawn. The sidecar has to choose (snap as if its own camera were active, and say so) rather than pretend the disc decides.
- The `mHeight -= 1` in alive's PS1 decoder is a hack on a demo function. Do not ship a byte-reproducible atlas on it until retail frames decode clean without it or the extra row is explained.
- Tints and shadow zones are colour the CLUT does not carry; a sprite pass that skips them is wrong for most Exoddus creatures and reads as right.
- The stamp evicts everyone: batch atlas changes with camera rebuilds, never re-emit an atlas alone.
- Demo paths carry sprites like any other; `showDemoPaths` and the `-demos` export marker gate them the way they gate markers.
- The per-type rules are the same shape as the destination rules in `extra.js`, and the same trap: a rule ported from one game's code applied to the other's (Oddysee's window centre offset, Exoddus's half-scale pixel) is off by a few units everywhere and looks aligned at moderate depth, exactly what the alignment anchors exist to catch.

## Ruled out

- **An engine trace or relive in WebAssembly as the feature's motion.** Each is above; both need PC data and a player, and either would record one play. The hand-ported brains were ruled out beside them while the port looked like thousands of lines of player-relative code a creature; under the no-player reading it is the route that shipped. If a recording is ever wanted it is a separate item that says "recording" on its face.
- **Copying PC frame-table offsets onto PS1 files.** They differ between the two PC games already and the frame data precedes the tables; the key is file, resource id and ordinal, validated on the disc.
- **Drawing sprites under or over the fg pass as it stands.** The pass is a tinted diagnostic over a flattened four-layer mask, and correct compositing needs the per-layer split, which is a disc rebuild. What shipped draws the sprites under an untinted copy of that same flattened mask: right in Oddysee, whose mask layers all sit above its sprites, and approximate under the two Exoddus layers that sit below them, which the geometry doc states.
- **Putting the sprite reference into `map_data`.** It would change the data file on every rule fix; a sidecar of its own keeps the data file stable and is fetched beside the first paint, which draws the artwork without waiting for it.
- **Loading the sprite modules on demand.** A dynamic `import()` the first time the toggle came on would keep them out of the initial load, but with the objects shown by default the modules are needed at boot, and the import graph stays the one `imports.test.js` reads.
