# 106. Every file of the site in the Wayback Machine, each deploy consistent with itself

**Status:** undecided · the plan below awaits a verdict; nothing is built, and the one capture made so far was the premise test · **Effort:** medium (one stdlib Python tool, two jobs in the CI workflow and a scheduled audit; an Internet Archive account and two repository secrets) · **Where:** `.github/workflows/ci.yml` and a new `tools/archive_site.py` with its tests

**Filed:** 2026-11-02, from a survey of what the archive holds of the site, a test of how its replay finds a file, and a reading of the Save Page Now API document and the archive's help pages.

## What and why

An archived copy of the map is only as complete as what the capturing browser happened to fetch. The page, the stylesheet's partials and every module load at boot, so every capture has them; the artwork loads per view and the other game's data behind the first, so a capture holds the opening screen's cameras and little else. Measured on 2026-11-02, the archive holds 1,885 of the site's 8,900 files: every one of the 1,758 base cameras (a long browsing session on 2026-08-18 fetched them), 16 of the 1,433 foreground masks, 2 of the 100 sprite sheets, none of the 5,470 release art files and none of the fourteen release overlays. A visitor to an archived copy sees the first screen and then blanks.

The live site stays exactly as it is: nothing may fetch more, preload more or load in another order. What changes is on the archive's side, so the job is to get every file archived by name and to keep each deploy's files consistent with each other there.

## Findings

### How the replay finds a file

The premise, tested 2026-11-02: when an archived page requests a file, the archive serves the capture of that URL nearest in time to the page's own timestamp, from whichever session made it, so a file archived alone is found by every archived copy of the page.

- The capture of the map taken at 11:30:30 UTC on 2026-11-02, after *Keep opener free at the modules' top level for the Wayback Machine* (`ea51a1a9`) deployed at 09:57 UTC, renders; its seventy modules all resolve to their own session, and its data and cameras to whichever session holds the nearest copy: Oct 7, Oct 14, Oct 31 and its own.
- Paramonia path 9 has two cameras, archived since Oct 7, and four masks, none archived. Opened at `#AO/F1/9`, that capture drew both cameras and asked for all four masks, which answered 404 although every one of them is on the live site: the replay fetches nothing from the live web.
- `cams/ao/F1/F1P09C01_fg37.png` was saved alone through `https://web.archive.org/save/<url>`, anonymously, at 13:37:38 UTC; the index row appeared within twenty seconds, its digest the file's own SHA-1.
- The same page capture, opened again at 13:42, drew that mask from the 13:37:38 capture, two hours after its own timestamp, and still got 404 for the other three. A probe under a minute after the save still saw 404: the replay lags a fresh capture by one to five minutes.
- A revisit record (a re-capture whose bytes equal an earlier one; the index shows status `-` and mimetype `warc/revisit`) replays as the full file: asked under the Oct 14 page's timestamp, `R1P15C01.png` came back from its Oct 14 revisit row, byte-identical to the tree.
- The archive's replay code and its descendants agree: the capture with the smallest absolute time difference wins, in either direction, with no cutoff (a request under the year 2000 was answered from 2026) and no notion of session; the chosen capture is named in the redirect's `x-archive-redirect-reason` header.

### What a page capture misses

The Oct 31 and Nov 2 captures of the map, both Save Page Now jobs, recorded none of ten JSON files the page fetches at boot (`map_data_*`, `glossary_*`, `messages_*`, `enum_labels_*`, `field_types_*`), while recording the four JSON files that had changed since the Oct 14 capture and dozens of unchanged cameras. The replay serves those ten from Oct 14, and the Nov 2 page survives only because the dataset has not changed since Oct 13. The Save Page Now changelog explains part of it: the service re-archives a page's embedded resources at most every 40 minutes, and stylesheets, scripts and fonts only every 6 hours, so a page captured soon after a deploy can sit beside the modules of the deploy before. Whatever the full cause, a page capture does not reliably carry even the files the page loads at boot, so files are submitted by name.

### The trap, measured

Nothing but the sprite sheets and the release art carries a content hash in its name, so an archived page and the files it loads can come from different deploys. The nearest-capture rule puts every page capture in the second half of the gap between two deploys at risk: a file the next deploy changes has its new capture nearer than its old one, unless an old capture sits nearer still. The dataset is the live example: last captured Oct 14, last changed Oct 13; had a deploy on Nov 3 changed its format, every page capture from Oct 29 on would have been served the new file.

Churn, over the 57 successful deploys from 2026-09-08 to 2026-11-02: a deploy changes or adds a median of 3 unhashed files (mean 52, max 1,450; 16 deploys changed none, 53 of 57 changed thirty or fewer; the three that changed more were artwork rebuilds). Hashed files are only ever added: 5,582 over seven deploys, 5,470 of them in the releases deploy. Deploys bunch: 7 of 22 deploy days had more than two, the busiest eleven; 23 of the 56 gaps between deploys were under ten minutes and 32 under six hours; 18 file-days in eight weeks saw one unhashed file change in three or more deploys the same day, `changelog.json` and `index.html` most often. The tree is 365.8 MB: 139 unhashed code, page and data files (11.2 MB), 3,191 unhashed artwork files (205.1 MB), 5,570 hashed files (149.5 MB).

### What the archive offers

The Internet Archive documents no way for an owner to have a site crawled: its help pages say there is no "crawl my site now", that Save Page Now saves one page and enrols it in no crawl, and point organisations at Archive-It. What exists, read on 2026-11-02:

- **Save Page Now 2**, the API behind the save form, documented only in a Google Doc by the archive's Vangelis Banos ([the API document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Nsv52MvSjbLb2PCpHlat0gkzw0EvtSgpKHu4mk0MnrA/mobilebasic), updated 2026-07-22, and [its changelog](https://docs.google.com/document/d/19RJsRncGUw2qHqGGg9lqYZYf7KKXMDL1Mro5o1Qw6QI/mobilebasic)). Authentication is the account's S3-style key pair from archive.org/account/s3.php, sent as `authorization: LOW key:secret`; anonymous saves still work but are counted by IP, and GitHub's runners share theirs. One URL per request: `POST https://web.archive.org/save` returns a job id, `GET /save/status/<id>` answers pending, success or error (results kept about an hour; the success lists the embeds captured, at most 500). Options worth having: `force_get=1` for a file that is not a page (a faster queue with no browser), `skip_first_archive=1`, and `if_not_archived_within`, which defaults to one hour: a URL captured less than an hour ago is not captured again, the old capture is returned instead, so a re-capture after a deploy must set it low. Limits: 7 captures a minute and 30,000 a day for an authenticated account (3 and 200 anonymous), the same URL at most 5 times a day (the error text says 10; one third-party report met a cap of 1 "for that Resource type" on a PDF), 5 GB of captured data a day, 2 GB per file, a 10 s connection timeout, and above 20 captures of one host in a minute each new one waits a fifth of a second per capture in flight. Captures land in the public Wayback Machine at once unless `delay_wb_availability=1`. The archive's September 2026 protections answer traffic they take for automated with 429s, and the document's figures have moved four times since 2023; more quota is by mail to info@archive.org.
- **Bulk routes under the same account**: [Save Page Now for Google Sheets](https://archive.org/services/wayback-gsheets/), 5,000 URLs a sheet against the same 30,000 a day, each row written back with its capture or error, one sheet at a time, defaulting to a 6 hour skip and a delayed availability of about 10 hours, with a third-party walkthrough of 2026-02 warning that captures can take days or weeks to show; and mail to spn@archive.org, the first 300 links of each message, 10 messages a day. Both are driven by hand.
- **Archive-It**: the archive's subscription service, priced by data budget and by quote (a Canadian consortium lists $2,100 a year for 0.125 TB), with a free Sponsored tier by application for non-institutional efforts. It takes a sitemap as a seed, its public collections surface in web.archive.org about a day after capture, and its crawls are scheduled in its own interface, never from a deploy.
- **ArchiveBot**: ArchiveTeam's volunteer crawler, not the archive's. A channel operator can feed it a file of URLs (`!ao < file`), non-recursive, no JavaScript, and its WARCs reach the Wayback Machine through ArchiveTeam's collections, two days or so to an item on one measured job. Its wiki says it does not run crawlers on behalf of other crawlers and guarantees nothing.
- **Not routes**: a self-made WARC reaches the Wayback Machine only with the archive's approval, which ArchiveTeam's FAQ says a user is unlikely to get; Cloudflare's Always Online, which the hostname could use since it is proxied by Cloudflare, shares only a site's popular pages every 30 days on the free plan; the browser extension saves one tab; the archive's own wide crawls run a year or more apart and reach nothing a script loads.

### Serving details that bear on it

- The hostname is proxied by Cloudflare in front of GitHub Pages. Cloudflare caches `.png`, `.js` and `.css` at its edge (`cf-cache-status` MISS and HIT seen; the client was told `max-age=14400` until 2026-11-10, when the zone was set to respect the origin's headers) and passes `.json` and HTML through; the origin sends `max-age=600`. After a deploy, the edge nearest the archive's crawler may hand it the outgoing bytes of a changed file for the edge's TTL. Cloudflare also injects its own scripts into the HTML, which the archive captures along with everything else.
- GitHub Pages gzips text, publishes a deploy within up to ten minutes, and re-stamps every file's `Last-Modified` and `ETag` on every deploy. The replay keeps the original headers as `x-archive-orig-*`, so a `HEAD` of a capture's `id_` form reads `x-archive-orig-last-modified` and says which deploy served it, without a download.
- The index's `digest` is the SHA-1 of the stored payload: a PNG's own SHA-1, but for gzipped text the SHA-1 of the compressed bytes, stable across captures of the same bytes (one module kept one digest over nine deploys) and never equal to the tree's. Fetching `/web/<timestamp>id_/<url>` with compression accepted and decoding gives the file: `js/main.js`, `css/main.css`, `annotations.json` and `map_data_ao.json` fetched that way were byte-identical to the tree.
- The index lags and flickers: a domain-wide query on 2026-11-02 omitted the Oct 29 index capture and most of the Oct 31 and Nov 2 rows that exact-URL queries returned, and the help centre says a fresh capture can be visible for hours or days, vanish, and return once a longer-term index holds it. A coverage count is a lower bound and a missing row is not a missing capture; the replay's own redirect is the authority on what a page is served.
- The archive refuses connections from a client that asks too fast: a dozen quick requests in ten seconds was enough on 2026-11-02, and the availability API answered 429 after a handful. Unofficial figures put the index API at 15 to 30 requests a minute.
- Modules replay under the archive's own `jm_` mode, which prepends `var` declarations of the eight globals, while classic scripts are wrapped in a `{ let … }` block; the archive's module support is an open issue on its tracker and mid-rollout by its staff's account, so whether an archived module runs is worth re-checking after each of its changes. The archive's `sw_` replies carry no `Service-Worker-Allowed` header, so an archived page cannot register `sw.js` even if a visitor turns the setting on.

## Options

What each costs once and on every deploy, what it promises, what it leaves to chance, and whether it runs from the workflow without touching the deploy:

- **A. One Save Page Now job per file, from the workflow.** Once: an account, two secrets, a small client, and a first pass of about 7,100 files at 7 a minute, some 17 hours across three or four scheduled runs. Per deploy: the changed unhashed files, median 3, twice each, plus the page and any new hashed files; a minute or two of the archive's time, nothing of the deploy's. Promises: one job and one verification per URL, so the ledger is exact and a stale copy is caught. Leaves to chance: the per-URL daily cap on a file changed three or more times in a day, index flicker, 429s and outages, which the next run picks up. Runs from the workflow, after the deploy, never gating it. Consistency: the tightest window of any option, with the pre-deploy capture below the only mechanism that protects page captures made by others.
- **B. Pages visitors never see that reference every file.** About a hundred files a page, 89 pages, each one capture; 13 minutes a pass. Promises a whole set in one session. Leaves to chance: the 50 second browser window after which "partial success may still be recorded" with no partial status, so a truncated capture reads as success and is caught only by diffing its embed list, capped at 500, against the page; the 40 minute and 6 hour embed windows, which skip exactly the files a deploy changed when anything captured them recently; a fixed URL's five captures a day; and the pages themselves, generated and committed in the tree. Runs from the workflow. No precedent found. Cannot do the pre-deploy capture, since the outgoing files are no longer listed anywhere once the new tree exists.
- **C. Sitemap or outlink capture.** No archive intake reads a sitemap. `capture_outlinks=1` follows at most 100 ranked links from a page, each its own job with its own timestamp, reusing outlinks captured elsewhere rather than re-capturing them. B with worse timestamps.
- **D. Archive-It.** A consistent crawl of the whole site per run, taking the sitemap as a seed, public in a day. Paid or Sponsored by application, scheduled by hand, never deploy-triggered, and 367 MB a crawl against a data budget.
- **E. ArchiveBot or asking the archive.** Free, a file of URLs, two days or more to replay, a volunteer's judgment, no automation and no repeat; at most a one-off first pass if the operators agree. Mail to info@archive.org raises quotas; nothing found says it crawls a site on request.
- **F. The Google Sheets service.** Two sheets cover Oddworld's tree by hand, with a result per row and no code; days to weeks to show, a skip window and a delayed availability to switch off, and nothing for the deltas. A fallback for the first pass if the API's quota proves tighter than its document says.
- **G. Nothing.** The archive's own crawls and other people's saves keep the opening screen and whatever someone browsed: today's 1,885 of 8,900.

## Sketch

Option A, as one tool, `tools/archive_site.py`, run by two jobs around each deploy and by a schedule, with Save Page Now 2 as the only way anything reaches the archive and the tree as the only list of what should be there.

**What is submitted.** Every file under `public/` is one of three kinds, told apart by its path alone: hashed (the sprite sheets and the release art, whose names carry their pixels), unhashed artwork (the cameras and masks, which change only on a rebuild), and the rest (the page, the level pages, the stylesheet, the modules, the worker, the data files). A hashed file is submitted once, when it first appears, and never again: a file with that name never changes. An unhashed file is submitted whenever a deploy changes it, twice: once before the deploy from the still-live site, so the archive holds the outgoing bytes under a timestamp just before the switch, and once after, verified against the new tree. The page is submitted after every deploy, last, so the archive carries a rendering copy per deploy whose nearest neighbour for every file is that deploy's own.

**Why twice.** The replay serves each file from the capture nearest the page's timestamp, in either direction. A page captured by anyone between two deploys finds, for a file the later deploy changes, the later deploy's capture nearer than the file's old one unless an old copy sits nearer still. Capturing the outgoing version minutes before the switch puts that copy there: for any page timestamp before the switch the outgoing copy is nearer than the incoming one, and for any after, the incoming one is. An outgoing copy whose bytes equal an earlier capture costs the archive a revisit record. The pre-capture uses the service's own one-hour default: a file captured within the hour already has its anchor, and skipping it spares the per-URL daily cap. The post-capture forces a fresh capture with `if_not_archived_within=1`. The window left uncovered is the deploy itself, the minutes between the pre-capture and the switch, during which the live site is itself in flux.

**The jobs.**
- `archive-outgoing` runs on every push to `main` beside the check jobs and needs none of them, with `continue-on-error: true` and a timeout. It reads the last deployed commit from the `github-pages` deployments API, lists the unhashed files `git diff --name-only --diff-filter=d` says the push changes, and submits each with `force_get=1` and the one-hour default; for each capture it reads `x-archive-orig-last-modified` off the replay and logs a capture whose stamp is not the previous deploy's as "not the outgoing bytes" rather than trusting it. The deploy job does not wait for it.
- `archive` needs `deploy`, runs only when it succeeded, with `continue-on-error: true` and a timeout, and starts by waiting out the origin's `max-age` so Cloudflare's edge has let the outgoing bytes go. It submits the changed unhashed files and the new hashed ones, verifies each capture's stamp against the deploy's (a PNG also by its index digest), resubmits a stale one after a wait, and submits the page last, once the files verify. It ends with a step summary: submitted, verified, skipped, failed, and the archive URL of the page capture.
- `archive-audit` runs on a schedule and by hand. It lists the tree at the deployed commit, asks the archive what it holds, submits whatever is missing (the first pass is this job run until it is done), and for the latest deploy's page capture asks the replay which capture each unhashed file resolves to and reads its stamp. It fails when the archive lacks a file or serves another deploy's bytes, which is the notice: a red scheduled run and its mail, never a red deploy. Optionally it opens the page capture in the browser suite's Chromium and counts the 404s and the foreign stamps, which also catches the archive changing how it replays a module.

**Pacing and failure.** One submission every nine seconds and a few jobs in flight, honouring a 429, a refused connection or the service's own error codes with a wait of minutes, stopping at the job's time budget and leaving the rest to the next run, and never exiting non-zero inside the deploy workflow. A timeout from the service is "outcome unknown", checked by a replay lookup before any resubmission, since the service has been seen to store a capture it reported as failed. The credentials are the account's key pair, read from `IA_ACCESS_KEY` and `IA_SECRET_KEY` in the environment, provided by repository secrets; the audit's read-only half runs without them.

## Verify

- The premise test above, repeated after the first `archive` run: the deploy's page capture opened at a path whose artwork no earlier session holds draws every camera and mask from captures within minutes of its own timestamp.
- The audit's own check: for the latest deploy, every unhashed file's nearest capture to the page capture carries the deploy's stamp, and every file in the tree has a capture.
- A deploy that changes a data file, watched both ways: the page capture before the deploy still resolves the file to the outgoing bytes, the page capture after to the incoming ones.
- Before the tool is built, three captures under the account settle what the document leaves open: a PNG captured twice within an hour with `if_not_archived_within=1` (does the second record appear, and is the per-URL cap 5, 10 or 1 for an image), a module the same way (is a direct capture of a script held to the six-hour window that embeds are), and a changed module captured one and ten minutes after a real deploy (does Cloudflare's edge hand the archive the old bytes, and for how long).

## Watch out

- Cloudflare's edge can serve a changed `.png`, `.js` or `.css` stale to the archive's crawler for its TTL after a deploy; the post-deploy job waits and verifies rather than trusts. The purge [105](item-105-purge-edge-at-deploy.md) proposes after each deploy shortens that wait to the seconds between the deploy and the purge.
- If a direct capture of a script turns out to be held to the six-hour embed window, the pre-capture of scripts and stylesheets is dropped, since it would block the post-capture that matters, and consistency for a module changed within six hours of its last capture waits for the window; 32 of the last 56 deploy gaps were shorter than that.
- The per-URL daily cap: a file changed in three or more deploys on one day (18 file-days in eight weeks) runs out of captures, and its last version waits for the next day's audit; pages captured in between get the version before. If the cap proves to be one a day for images, the pre-capture goes and the post-capture keeps the budget.
- The index lags the replay by minutes for a fresh capture and the domain-wide query lags exact lookups by hours or days, so "missing" is trusted only from an exact lookup or the replay's redirect, and the audit resubmits an unhashed file only when two runs agree.
- A page capture by anyone else during the deploy window itself can still straddle it; nothing on the archive's side prevents that, and the audit would show it as a foreign stamp for that capture only.
- Everything rests on a Google Doc whose limits have moved four times since 2023 and on a module replay mode the archive is still rolling out; the tool paces from the service's answers rather than from constants, and the audit's browser check is what notices the archive changing under it.
- Secrets never enter the tree; the jobs run only on `main`, and a pull request from a fork has no access to them.

## Ruled out

- Changing what the live page loads, preloads or references: the brief's one hard constraint, and the archive's problems are the archive's.
- A WARC made here and uploaded: it does not enter the Wayback Machine without the archive's say-so.
- Cloudflare Always Online: popular pages only, every 30 days, nothing a script loads.
- Relying on page captures to carry the boot-time files: the Oct 31 and Nov 2 captures show they do not.
- Query strings as cache busters on the archive's side: the index keys on the full URL, so a file fetched as `name?v=…` is archived under a name the page never asks for.

## The decision to make

Whether to build option A as sketched, and in which order: the three experiments need the account first, and the first pass needs nothing but the tool and a few scheduled runs. What only the owner can do: create or choose the archive.org account and read its two keys off archive.org/account/s3.php, add them as `IA_ACCESS_KEY` and `IA_SECRET_KEY` to the repository, approve the three experiments (each a handful of captures beyond the premise test), and decide [105](item-105-purge-edge-at-deploy.md), whose purge is what lets the post-deploy job stop waiting out the edge.
