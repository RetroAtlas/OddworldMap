# The PlayStation's own code

- The decomp is the PC build's source, so where the viewer has to move as the console does, the routine is read off the disc ([format-gotchas.md](format-gotchas.md) records what differed). A reading is a claim until the disc's code has run beside it, and the tools here are that run, in three links: an interpreter of the console's processor, a model of the routine in Python that the interpreter's run of the disc's code is compared with, and the model's runs, which the viewer's unit test holds the JavaScript port to. The comparison needs a disc and is run by hand; the unit suites need none, and hold the port's numbers to the model's over the tables the viewer ships.

## The interpreter

- [tools/oddmap/r3000.py](../tools/oddmap/r3000.py) is the console's two megabytes of RAM at `0x80000000`, its registers and the instructions compiled code is made of. `load` puts bytes where the console loads them, `call` runs a function with up to four arguments to its return and gives what it leaves in `v0`, and the readers and writers (`r8` to `s32`, `w8` to `w32`) lay out and read back whatever the function works on.
- An executable's text loads at the address its header names and an overlay at the one its message table gives (`Image` in [exe.py](../tools/oddmap/exe.py) reads either by address). An Oddysee overlay opens 0x68 bytes before the executable's file ends, over its padding, so the executable is loaded first.
- It runs compiled code and nothing else, which is what lets it leave out the load delay (a compiler never reads a register in the instruction after its load), the overflow traps and a branch in another's delay slot. There are no coprocessors, no hardware registers, no interrupts and no BIOS: an instruction or an address it does not have is a `Fault` naming the instruction's address, as are a word off its boundary and a run past `limit` instructions.
- `stubs` cuts a routine off from the rest of the game: where the code calls or jumps to an address named there, the caller's own function answers, given the machine once the delay slot has run, and what it returns is `v0`, or `RUN` to let the function run after all, its arguments seen. The function `call` is handed always runs, the stubs are the ones set as the call begins, and a stub may not call in its turn. Which callees run as the disc's code and which are answered is the port's decision: the engine's arithmetic runs, and what reaches for the hardware or the objects around it is answered.
- A set in `stores` gathers every address the code stores to, which is how the globals a routine writes are found without reading for them.
- An instruction is decoded once, into a function, and decoded again if anything is stored or loaded over it. A run is a few million instructions a second.
- `text_codes` in exe.py keeps a reader of its own for the fonts' measuring routines: a leaf run over the memory it is handed, refusing any read beyond it, which a whole machine of zeroed RAM would let through.
- `tools/tests/test_r3000.py` holds every instruction to what the console does with it, bar the load delay and the trap left out, over hand-assembled code. A hint fly's run executes thirty-five of its fifty-six instructions, so for the rest that file is all there is.

## The hint fly's model and its check

- [tools/oddmap/hintfly.py](../tools/oddmap/hintfly.py) holds the model: a `World` of what the swarm shares with the game (the frame counter, the chant, the sounds asked for, the dice and the two cursors into them), and a `HintFly` built as the constructor leaves one, with `update` a tick and `sprites` the corners the render draws from. Its fields carry the names the check compares by. The dice, the alphabet and the messages are a disc's own (`tables`), as are the three numbers a word is laid out and held by, read off its code (`spelling`): a step of a letter's grid, a letter's cell and the hold. The engine's quarter wave and the routines' other numbers, the swarm at rest and the pause before a word among them, are the model's constants, as they are the viewer's, so a disc where one differed would fail the check rather than pass on a number of its own.
- No address is written down. `code` finds the routines in an overlay by their shape, since each of the base disc's eleven overlays links them somewhere else and a release links its executable differently too: the routines that spell build the alphabet's address less the first letter's code; of those the update is the one a table of virtual functions names, with the render in the slot after it; the update calls the one that forms a word; its other callees are the flies' tick and, making no call, a fly's setup; and the one that measures the longest word is the speller called by what also sets a fly up, the constructor. Outside the overlay the update calls only the event query and loads only the frame counter, and the render calls the engine's drawing and loads one pointer. The flies' tick passes the literal 60 to the routines that make a sound, which all call the engine's player; of what the player calls, the one the flies' routines call too is the roll of the dice and the rest are the voices it plays on, and the one word it loads is where its sounds are kept. A shape that is off in one of those is refused by naming it. The sound routines are gathered only to reach the player, and of the engine's drawing only the call handed each fly's sprite matters to the check, its loss showing as sprites that never come.
- `spelling` reads the numbers off the routines `code` finds. The routine that forms a word calls the roll of the dice and one other routine of the executable, the engine's product, which it hands a step of a letter's grid, one constant at both its calls, for a dot's column and for its row; of what it steps a register on by in place, the stack and the address of the alphabet apart, the one number above one is a letter's cell; and the one number the update adds to the frame counter between forming a word and the next jump is the hold. A shape that is off in one of those is refused by the part as well. `spellings` gives them for every overlay of a disc, which is how the builder reads a release's ([builder.md](builder.md#releases)).
- [tools/hintfly_verify.py](../tools/hintfly_verify.py) runs the two side by side. Its `Console` loads the executable and one overlay, lays the object out in spare RAM as the constructor leaves it (the constructor itself is the engine's for the most part and is not run: the flies' block, the point, the message, then the disc's own measure of the longest word and setup of the twenty), and answers three kinds of call: the event query with the chant, the voices with none free, and the engine's drawing by recording what it is handed. The dice roll, the two waves, the fixed-point product, the sound routines and the engine's player run as the disc's code, the player seen as it is called. The two cursors are found by what the routines store to: the one byte outside its fly that a fly's setup writes, and the one byte a roll does.
- After every tick it compares every field of the object and of every fly the swarm has reached, both cursors, each sound's volume and the pitch given it, and each sprite's corner in the order the engine is handed them, and names the first that differs with its message and tick.
- A run is every overlay the paths load that holds an alphabet, over every message that alphabet spells, from cursors, at a point and under a chant drawn from `--seed`. Each message is first spelled through under a held chant, on to the word of nothing the console forms with twelve flies once the message is over and to a second, formed with no sizing left to do, then run for `--ticks` more under a chant that comes and goes, which is what breaks it in every state and lets go of words as they stand. What a run meets too seldom to count on is put there. The chant is broken once on a tick the swarm reaches a word's size, where the console lets go before it would form. And the last eight flies of every word, never the first, which is the one that sounds, are stood by their dots: four exactly a pixel off, either way across and down, which keep the speed they formed with and fly on past, and four a fraction nearer, which arrive. Each overlay's line counts the words formed and the ones let go of, and says whether all eight changes of state were seen and by what numbers it spelled.
- `python3 tools/hintfly_verify.py` takes the disc from `$ODDWORLD_DISC_AO`, or a release's image by `--disc`, spreads the messages over the machine's cores and exits non-zero where an overlay differs or will not run; `--overlay` and `--message` narrow it. The base disc is 3.1 billion instructions, a minute and a half on a machine of fourteen cores. Every Oddysee disc has been run, the base's and the eight releases' ([releases.json](../tools/data/releases.json)), and each agrees in all of its eleven overlays, on the base's numbers but for Abe a GoGo, which agrees on its own; each runs all 36 messages but the Spanish edition, which leaves unrun the one whose comma its alphabet has no letter for.
- What a pass leaves open: two steps of the console's that change nothing in any state a run reaches, the scatter stopping at the first fly already looping and the flies a word leaves over being set looping as it forms, which the model keeps because the disc has them; and what no message run on any disc reaches, a letter outside the alphabet, a word of nothing inside a message and arithmetic past its range, which `tools/tests/test_hintfly.py` holds instead.
- What it does not hold: that a path's overlay builds the object at all, which is its factory table's matter; where the constructor puts the point; the event that marks the object dead, which the update asks for before all else and the check answers with none; a sprite's frame, layer and blend, the render being compared by its corners alone; what the voices do with a sound; and that the tables the viewer ships are the disc's, which is the build's.
- `tools/tests/test_hintfly.py` holds the model to hand-worked runs over a three-letter alphabet, by the base's numbers and by others, and `code` and `spelling` to a synthetic overlay and executable and their refusals. They guard the model against a slip; only the disc proves it.

## The runs the port is held to

- [tools/hintfly_trace.py](../tools/hintfly_trace.py) prints a run of the model as JSON, over the tables the viewer ships for the base game, with `--release` for a release of it, the numbers it spells by among them, or with `--disc` over a disc's own. `tests/unit/hintfly.test.js` holds the viewer's brain to such runs: the test's `run(hint(id, at, screen, seed, tables), ticks, flips, shots)` is `hintfly_trace.py <id> <ticks> --chant <flips> --shots <shots> --seed <seed>`, with `--at` the hint's point less its screen's corner and `--release jp` where the tables are the Japanese release's, whose dots, cells and hold are its own; `line`, `sum`, `rolls`, `own`, `dice` and `shots` mean the same on both sides and the test's `words` are the ticks of the tool's; and the two swarms at rest are `--shots` with no `--chant`, under any message.
- The chant it runs under is the map's and lives in the tool, not the model: a hint with no message hears none, and one that has spelled every word of its message lets go by itself as the last word's hold ends and is heard again once the swarm is at rest, where the console's goes on while Abe chants. The frame a fly's sprite shows, its layer and its blend are the viewer's own too, and come from no run.
- `PortLiterals` in `tools/tests/test_hintfly.py` makes every run the JavaScript test makes, the hint of no message over a table of one empty message and the hint of one number of its own over the base's tables with that number, and looks for each number in the test's source, where it has to stand once and whole, so the Python suite fails when the model and the test's literals part, with no disc. The test's `run` calls are counted against its `RUNS`, which a run added to the test joins. `rolls` folds both dice cursors after every tick because where they end hides a roll that differs and is made up later.
- A change to how the console's hint fly is read goes model first, then the check against the disc, then the runs printed and the test's literals replaced, and the port last.

## The falling item, the render and their check

- [tools/oddmap/fallingitem.py](../tools/oddmap/fallingitem.py) models the falling item's constructor, which is what decides where one waits: a `World` of what it shares with the game (the level and path, the frame counter, whether an item already hums for the rest) and a `FallingItem` as the constructor leaves one, at its record's corner whatever the camera. The update, and with it the rumble, the fall and the smash, is not modelled.
- [tools/oddmap/render.py](../tools/oddmap/render.py) models what a waiting item shows, which is no matter of the item's: both games draw every animated object through one routine of the executable, the one an object's table of virtual functions names in the slot after its update. `bounding` is the frame's rect stood at the object, `side` where the engine finds that rect by the camera's window, `quad` the corners the drawing hands the GPU, and `render` the three as the routine runs them, a quad or nothing. A `Sprite` is what the routine reads of an object and a `Stage` what it shares with the game, with the one difference between the games' drawing, that Oddysee's upends no frame. [format-gotchas.md](format-gotchas.md) has where the decomp's drawing is the PC build's.
- No address is written down here either. `fallingitem.code` takes the update to be the one routine of an overlay that builds both the gain of a fall and the multiplier its hum's period compiles to, the second of which Oddysee's meat saw builds too; a table of virtual functions names it, with the executable's render in the slot after; and of the routines that build that table's address the constructor is the one that reads through its second argument, a record. Of the words the constructor loads by name, the one outside the overlay is the frame counter and the one inside names the item that hums. `render.code`, handed the render, takes the test of where a rect lies to be its one callee that makes a single call, the event query, and the drawing to be the one that makes calls and is called again by another callee, the shadow's render, its last call handing a quad to the ordering table; the address the render hands the test is the map's, and the one word it loads is the pointer it finds the camera through. A shape that is off is refused by naming the part.
- [tools/fallingitem_verify.py](../tools/fallingitem_verify.py) runs the two beside the disc's code, an overlay at a time with its disc's executable: every overlay that holds an update, eleven of Oddysee's and fifteen of Exoddus's over its two discs. The constructor runs with the two constructors beneath it, and every other call the three make is answered, among them the object's entry in the game's list, the animation the item asks for, its setup and its shadow. It builds every record the game places, 21 and 93, read off the path chunks with the camera whose list holds each, and a record whose camera is not the one the builder's rule files it under is refused. Each is built under its own camera and under another, in four levels of which two are Oddysee's farms and one its lines, with and without an item already humming, and every field the model names is compared, with what the constructor stores to outside its object and the table of functions it leaves there.
- The item so built is then drawn by the executable's render, standing in every frame of the chunk of animations its constructor asked for, which is found in the level archives by the table it was set up from and is one to an overlay whatever the level. What the render hands the ordering table is compared with the model's quad corner by corner, and the disc's own answer to where the frame's rect lies with the model's. The test and the drawing run as the disc's code, the event and the hand-over are answered, and so is everything else the render calls, the colour, the shadow and the dirty rects, with nothing.
- What placed records cannot be relied on to reach is put there. Three records are made up: at the other scale, at a scale that is neither, and with a rect left of the path's origin whose sides sum odd. And `render.by_hand` stands an object, frame by frame, either side of each edge of the window and off each of its corners, on whole and on half units, at its own scale and two others, mirrored each way; half a unit over the left and the top of a window away from the origin, where cutting the screen's position and cutting the world's part ways; in the window at each scale that puts a measure of the frame a 65536th either side of where its rounding carries, which is what holds a rounding's 0.499 to the bit; with its render's offsets moved; and hidden, made in another level, made in another path and under the event that ends a life. An overlay's line counts its records, builds, frames rendered and drawn, and scales, and says whether a rect was found on the window and on every side of it.
- `python3 tools/fallingitem_verify.py --game AO`, or `AE`, takes the discs from the builder's variables or from `--disc`, and exits non-zero where an overlay differs or will not run; `--overlay` narrows it. Oddysee's disc is 88 million instructions and 26 seconds and Exoddus's two are 127 million and 39, on one core, and those three are the discs run so far.
- What a pass leaves open: arithmetic past sixteen bits, which no path's coordinates reach; and a rounding's last bit where no frame has an odd measure for it, since only an odd one can be put a 65536th either side of a carry. Exoddus's art has one for each of its six roundings and Oddysee's for four of its five, every frame of its items being of an even height.
- What it does not hold: that a path's overlay builds the object at all; Exoddus's second constructor, which an explosion set makes its rubble with and is handed no record; which animation an overlay's items stand in, which is read off the constructor and compared with nothing; a frame's texture, its colour on the screen and its place among the layers, the render being compared by its quad's corners alone; what the GPU makes of the quad; and that the tables the viewer ships are the disc's, which is the build's.
- `tools/tests/test_render.py` and `tools/tests/test_fallingitem.py` hold the two models to frames and records worked by hand, `by_hand` to the places it is to reach, and the two finders to a synthetic executable and overlay and their refusals. They guard the models against a slip; only the disc proves them.

## The runs the falling item's port is held to

- [tools/fallingitem_trace.py](../tools/fallingitem_trace.py) prints the two models' run over the tables the viewer ships for a game, as JSON: every placed item under the camera the builder's rule files it under, which the check above holds to the discs' own lists, with `placed`, how many there are, `screens`, each screen a waiting item is drawn on with how many, and `gone`, each item drawn on some ticks of its wait and not on others, with those ticks. `--items` adds each item's point, its scale and the rows of its screen each frame of its two arts fills.
- Three readings in it are the map's and live in the tool, not the models: the wait (an item on the always-on switch rumbles in its second art from the first tick until its interval is out and any other stands in its first, a frame held for its animation's count of ticks), which art a level's items stand in, the viewer's rule, and the rows, the GPU's, which fills short of a quad's right and bottom edges.
- `PortLiterals` in `tools/tests/test_fallingitem.py` makes that run for both games and looks its numbers up in the port's tests, where each has to stand once and whole. In the sweep of `tests/unit/sprites.test.js` they are the window it watches, how many items each game places, the screens with their counts, the item that comes and goes with its ticks, Oddysee's render offset and the two placements pinned by their height. The gate's test in `tests/unit/fallingitem.test.js` stands an item of its own on a screen of its own, so the models are run over that item and screen, and the places either side of each edge, the pair at half scale, the frames a short frame leaves undrawn and Oddysee's offset are looked up the same way.
- What the port's tests hold of the fall, the smash and the debris is tied to nothing, the update being unmodelled. Neither are the layer and the colour `furniturerules.js` gives an item, which the constructor's model has from the disc and no test of the port holds.
- A change to how the console's falling item or its render is read goes as the hint fly's does: model first, then the check against the discs, then the runs printed and the tests' literals replaced, and the port last.

## The wells, their leaves and their check

- [tools/oddmap/well.py](../tools/oddmap/well.py) models a well's constructor and update and a leaf's: a `World` of what a camera's wells and their leaves share with the game (the dice and the two cursors into them, the wells' and the leaves', the path's lines, the camera's window, Abe's start, the switches written on and the list of objects), a `Well` as its constructor leaves one over a record, a `Leaf` as its constructor leaves one let go, and `update` a tick of either. A leaf's flight stands on two routines of the executable's that are no matter of the leaf's, and their models stand apart for the next port: the test of a ray against a path's lines, in 24.8 with every product and quotient cut toward zero, in [tools/oddmap/collide.py](../tools/oddmap/collide.py), and the quotient of two 16.16 numbers, in fixed.py beside the product. [format-gotchas.md](format-gotchas.md) has where the decomp's are the PC build's.
- `World.tick` is a pass of the engine's loop, which is no routine a check can call. Each executable's main loop walks the game's list of objects against the list's live size, so that an object made during a pass has its turn in it, passes over one that is dead or not to update, spends a tick of an object's delay or else calls its update, and takes the dead out afterwards, each one's place handed to the list's last. The model makes that pass over a list of its own and so does the check's console, so the order the leaves take their bytes of the dice in is that reading of the loop on both sides, compared with nothing.
- No address is written down. `well.code` takes the leaf's update to be the one routine of an overlay that hands a call 0xCCCC as its third argument and takes 127 from a number: the four fifths of its speed a leaf keeps, and the middle of a byte of jitter. A table of virtual functions names it, with the executable's render in the slot after; of the routines that build that table's address the leaf's constructor is the one another calls, and its caller is the well's update, which a table of its own names; of the routines that build that one's address the well's constructor is the one that calls into the overlay, its two inits. What they reach for in the executable is told by the order they call it in. The leaf's constructor makes eight calls: its base's constructor, the resource, the animation's setup, the product twice, the roll of the engine's dice twice, and the sound. Its update makes eight: the product twice, the quotient and the product, twice over, then the raycast and the test of a point by the camera's window, and the one word it loads from outside the overlay is the pointer to the path's lines. The well's update makes five: it opens on the event query, the one the render's test makes, and ends on the allocation and the leaf's constructor, and the switch states are the table a signed byte is loaded from by an index, by the update itself in Oddysee and in Exoddus by the one of its calls between that makes none. The one call the inits make beyond those is Exoddus's query of Abe's start, and each update loads one byte of its overlay by name, its cursor. A shape that is off is refused by naming the part. Eleven of Oddysee's overlays hold the routines and 53 of Exoddus's over its two discs, under 37 names.
- [tools/well_verify.py](../tools/well_verify.py) runs the model beside the disc's code a camera at a time: every well the camera's list holds, built by the overlay's constructor from its record's own bytes with the executable's constructors beneath it, then a pass of the loop for each of 1,024 ticks, four turns of the dice, each camera from the memory the disc loads, so that no run leans on the one before it. The updates and the leaf's constructor run as the disc's code, with the engine's product and quotient, its roll of the dice, its raycast and its test of a point. Answered are the event, with none; the allocation, with room of the tool's; the resource a leaf asks for, with the chunk of its animation, and the one a well asks for, with none, so that a well is built without its art; the animation's setup; the sound, the two rolls it is handed having run; the query of Abe's start, with the one given; and whatever else a constructor of the executable's calls.
- After the constructors and after every tick it compares both cursors, how many objects the list holds, each one's kind and its table of functions, and every field the model names: a well's scale, its switch, what it is told, its leaves' point and its exit's speeds, and a leaf's delay, place, speed, scale, layer, plane and tint, whether it has come to rest, the level and path it was made in and its render's offsets. Each leaf is then stood in the frame its age gives it and run through the executable's render, and whether the render goes on to the drawing is compared with `render.render`; the quad is not, Exoddus's leaf being cut ready from a sheet, a frame the drawing does not take as `render.Console` lays an animation out.
- A game's cameras are the ones whose wells let leaves go in a fresh game, 38 of Oddysee's and 126 of Exoddus's, read off the path chunks with the camera whose list holds each well, and each starts both cursors on the map's seed for it, the place among its path's objects of the first such well. A camera is run once: the cameras are dealt in turn over the overlays that hold the routines, so that every overlay runs a few, and `--overlay` runs them all in the ones it names.
- What placed records do not reach is put there by hand. A well of each type is run alone in its camera from a byte of the dice that lets a leaf go at once: with a leaf field set, each and both; told to let leaves go by 2 and by -1, and told not to; on the switch that always reads on, on another read off and written on, and on the table's last; at its other scale and at one that is neither; with its rect left of the path's origin, its sides an odd sum; in Exoddus with a leaf's point past sixteen bits from Abe's start; and, where the type has an exit, with one that leads down, at either scale, stands still, holds odd speeds or barely moves. Abe is started away from the path's origin under each, which Oddysee's wells are to take no notice of. Two more stand the window at the path's origin and let leaves go on its left edge and under its top, where a leaf within a unit past the origin is still on the window, its place being cut toward zero; a run made up for that is refused where none of its leaves gets there. And each disc's quotient and raycast are asked directly for what a flight cannot be relied on to throw them: the 320 pairs of `fixed.by_hand`, sizes under a whole and past it over divisors either side of 24 bits, up to a quotient past 32, under each pair of signs, and the 29 rays of `collide.by_hand`, across floors of both planes, a slope and a floor above zero, on a line's ends and a 64th off them, where a floor that starts at zero is landed on from before its start and one further on is not, and over lines of kinds past 15 and past 31, which a mask asks for by their low five bits.
- An overlay's line counts its cameras and the wells made up, their wells and ticks, the leaves let go, the ones come to rest and the ones ended on a floor of the plane in front, the ticks flown within a unit past the origin and the ticks drawn; a disc's line counts the pairs and rays it was asked.
- `python3 tools/well_verify.py --game AO`, or `AE`, takes the discs from the builder's variables or from `--disc`, spreads the cameras over the machine's cores and exits non-zero where a line differs or will not run; `--ticks` shortens the placed cameras' runs. Oddysee's disc is 410 million instructions and 14 seconds and Exoddus's two are 1.7 billion and 46, on a machine of fourteen cores, two minutes and nine of one core's time, and those three are the discs run so far.
- What a pass leaves open: an object dead before its turn in a pass, which the loop passes over and no well or leaf can be; a switch off the table's 256, where the console reads whatever lies there and the model refuses; and a leaf at a scale that is neither of the two a well has.
- What it does not hold: that a path's overlay builds a well at all, and the point its factory hands one, the record's corner and in Exoddus five lower, which is read off the factories and handed to both sides, Exoddus's lying in a second overlay loaded beside the first; the pass of the loop, as above; the cursors a camera starts from, which in the game are wherever the cameras before it left them; the engine's dice, whose two rolls for a leaf's sound run and are compared with nothing; the frame a leaf stands in, its texture and its colour on the screen; a well's own art; and that the tables the viewer ships are the disc's, which is the build's.
- `tools/tests/test_well.py` holds the model to wells and leaves worked by hand, `code` to a synthetic overlay and executable and their refusals, and what the tool adds to the model; `test_collide.py` and `test_fixed.py` hold the raycast and the quotient to rays and numbers worked by hand. They guard the models against a slip; only the disc proves them.

## The runs the wells' port is held to

- [tools/well_trace.py](../tools/well_trace.py) prints the model's runs over the tables the viewer ships for a game, as JSON. Given the game alone it watches every camera whose wells let leaves go for 1,024 ticks: `wells` and `cameras`, how many there are, `screens`, how many cameras hold one such well, two, three and four, `made`, the leaves let go, `flown`, how many leaves the ticks end with between them and the sums of their x and y, `never`, the wells none of whose leaves outlives the tick that makes it, `down`, the wells whose leaves leave downward or level, and `grounded`, the others one of whose leaves never rises above where it is let go. Given a level, a path and the places of wells among the path's objects, it prints those wells' own leaves at the ticks `--ticks` names, each its x and y in 16.16 in the order of the game's list, and by tick how many they are with the sums of their x and y.
- Three readings in it are the map's and live in the tool, not the model: a camera is read as freshly loaded, both cursors on the place among its path's objects of the first of its wells that lets leaves go, where an overlay starts them at 4 and 8; a well lets leaves go where a fresh game has it do so, told to and on no switch or the one that always reads on; and a leaf is its well's own, which the game's list of objects does not say.
- `PortLiterals` in `tools/tests/test_well.py` makes those runs and looks their numbers up in `tests/unit/well.test.js`, where each has to stand once and whole. Of the sweeps they are the run's length, the wells and the cameras, the leaves let go as forty a well, the screens by their wells, the three lists of wells, and every leaf at every tick, counted and its place summed, which a leaf flown otherwise on any camera would move; of the two tests of the console's own numbers, each flight the test reads move by move with the tick before a well's first leaf, the place a leaf is drawn at under Oddysee's offset, and the sums of the two cameras of several wells.
