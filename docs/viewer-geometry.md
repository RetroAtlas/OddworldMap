# Viewer: geometry, rendering and interaction

A camera's cell, its 368×240 window and the slack between windows are defined in [docs/format-gotchas.md](format-gotchas.md), and drawing code reads them off `geometry` and `LAYOUT`, never from per-game literals. Display against Settings is [docs/viewer-boot.md](viewer-boot.md)'s taxonomy.

## The canvas, the minimap and the export controls

- `#cv`'s `touch-action` is conditional on purpose: `none` normally, `pinch-zoom` while `body.page-zoomed` is set (`js/interaction.js` watches `visualViewport.scale`, and skips both its own pinch and a touch pointer's `setPointerCapture` in that state — capture would keep the gesture from reaching the browser, while a mouse keeps its capture or a drag released off the canvas never ends).
  - `body`'s `touch-action: manipulation` is the other half: it drops double-tap zoom, and with it the tap delay a zoomable page otherwise costs, while leaving pinch alone.
- `#minimap` paints inside [js/render.js](../public/js/render.js) at the end of `draw()` — a second, tiny projection of the same frame, so it can never lag the view — while [js/minimap.js](../public/js/minimap.js) holds only the scrubbing, through the same exported `minimapScale` factor the painter uses. It hides itself when the whole path fits the window, yields the bottom-right corner to the numbers panel via CSS (`:has`) — and on narrow to the screen list, a bottom sheet across the same corner there — and is deliberately not `.chrome`: an embed is exactly where orientation help is scarce.
- `#exportMenu` is the sidebar's one Export control: a `<details>` holding the three export buttons, closed at every boot and remembered nowhere, sitting after `#toggles` rather than inside it because an export is an action and not a Display flip. It opens on a click rather than a hover, since a hover has nothing for touch (the sidebar is a drawer on a phone, where the tooltips already have to filter hover events to the mouse) and nothing for the keyboard, where the summary is a disclosure button of the browser's own; and it stays open across a press, so a button's own progress label and dimming are in view while its export runs, which a menu closing on the click would hide. The world graph hides the two image exports inside it and leaves the disclosure standing for the third. The browser specs open it before they press.
- The JSON export button (`#exportJsonBtn`, in [js/export.js](../public/js/export.js)) hands the standing path over as the level editor's document. It is the one export the world-graph mode leaves standing, by the same line the Display toggles are on: its two neighbours would save a canvas nobody is looking at, while this one reads the path in hand, which the diagram is highlighting. It refuses rather than writing a file whose manifest holds a miss, because relive's importer aborts the process outright on a missing numeric property, and it says on the way out what the artefact costs: `ImportCameraAndFG1` removes every FG1 chunk unconditionally and re-adds one only from layers the JSON carries, so importing an art-free document strips the path's foreground masks.
  - Emitting the layers instead is not a builder change waiting on a rebuild, which is what this looked like from the import contract alone: relive's layer buffers are `u16[4][240][640]` and its masks are screen-space, while a PlayStation screen is 368 wide. What we could emit describes the narrower screen, so the masks cannot be derived from this data at any cost and the download says what it drops.

## The one painter and the whole-path export

- `paint` in [js/render.js](../public/js/render.js) is the one painter, and `draw()` is only the live canvas's call to it; [js/export.js](../public/js/export.js) calls it again into an offscreen canvas sized to a whole path, which is what "Export whole path" saves.
  - Its `transients` flag gates the six affordances that are the live view's alone — the flash ring, the dashed highlight, the edit selection's outline, a hovered Slig's pen, and the connection-arrow and wire spotlights. The two spotlights are why the flag exists rather than a note: each dims everything it is *not* focused on (a wire to 0.12, an arrow to 0.15), so exporting mid-hover would bake in a near-invisible overlay rather than a stray mark. The ruler and the route do render, being deliberate annotation.
  - `draw()` skips an image that has not arrived and repaints when it does, which is a hole in a paint that happens once. `preloadPath` waits for the artwork first, the foreground masks included whatever `show.fg` says — they are a percent of the bytes, and a toggle that moved mid-preload would otherwise draw masks nothing fetched.
  - Waiting is not sufficient, so `artworkReady` is asked again immediately before the paint: a load that failed reports itself `complete` with no width, and the image cache's own eviction can drop this path's entries while another selection passes through, leaving `img()` to mint blanks the paint silently skips. The same stretch re-checks the path — `paint` draws whatever is standing while the canvas was sized for what was preloaded, and a held `]` autorepeats through the window listener with the button disabled. Nothing may await between those checks and the paint.
  - `sizedCanvas` probes the canvas with one pixel painted and read back before the real paint. A browser past its limit says nothing: drawing into the canvas is ignored, or the size asked for is quietly clamped, and a blank canvas encodes to a perfectly good multi-megabyte PNG. Failing that, the area budget is quartered and the side halved and it asks again.
  - The budget (`EXPORT_MAX_PX`/`EXPORT_MAX_DIM`) follows the pitch on screen, which is what it is for: packed, the largest path in either game is 14.8 MP and nothing scales, while spaced AO lays its screens at the cell's own 1024x480 and the Credits path wants 49.2 MP. Past either bound the image scales to fit and says so.
  - The frame is the cell grid (`pathImage` sized on it, `cellOrigin()` anchoring it), so what the packed layout draws in the slack beyond the outermost screens falls outside the image — 248 AO markers and 526 collision-line pieces, none of them covering a screen. Nothing with screen coverage is ever cropped, pinned in `tests/unit/map-data.test.js`, and the spaced pitch takes that ground in.
  - The filename is read before the encode, not after: an encode this size is long enough for the place it names to move under it.

## The gaps toggle and the cell grid

- The "Gaps between screens" Display toggle (`setSpacing` in [js/state.js](../public/js/state.js), off by default, key `s`) lays screens at the cell's own pitch instead of packed edge to edge, so the slack shows at the size the game addresses it. The transform degenerates to `dX(wx) = wx - winX`, draw space becoming world space shifted, and a screen's interior stays 1:1 either way, which is what keeps a permalink's zoom meaning the same thing in both.
  - `LAYOUT` is `GEO` with `cellW`/`cellH` standing for the pitch in force. Anything reading a pitch reads that rather than the data's, which is why the live callers hand it to `screenRuns` and `lineRuns`: the dotting has to answer to how the map is laid out now, not to how it ships.
    - `imports.test.js` fails `model.js` reaching `state.js` by any chain, and names the chain: a model call answers for the layout and the place it is handed, never for the layout or selection that happens to be live. The display filter is the one live input it keeps: `snapTarget` asks `markerShown` and `PENS.on` the way the painter and the hover test do.
    - A geometry invariant is asked at both pitches (`pitches` in `tests/unit/fixtures.js`), because the two disagree in one direction only: a rule that collapses the slack unconditionally is exactly right packed and short by a slack per boundary spaced, so the packed half alone can never say so.
  - Flipping it moves every draw coordinate, so `setPitch` in [js/navigate.js](../public/js/navigate.js) brackets the flip: the view's centre (never its corner, which would slide the view), the ruler's ends and every route waypoint are read out as world points and put back after. A route survives it for the same reason a shared link does, its waypoints being world points either way.
  - The fold in `lineRuns` collapses only where the packing leaves it no draw space. Spaced out it is real canvas, so a line crossing the gap draws dotted across it rather than vanishing, and the marking keeps meaning what it meant: this is ground the game never renders.
    - Asked of the axes the fold crosses and no others. Along an axis it stays inside its window it travels real screen distance, which is none of the fold's, so a rule reading that axis keeps every descending line's crossing and packed draws it over the neighbour the fold lands on. Nor is the draw space worth measuring off the fold's own ends: a cut sits exactly on a cell boundary, `at(t)` puts it back an ulp low, and `Math.floor` hands back the cell before it.
  - A screen sits `winX`/`winY` inside its own cell, so `cellOrigin` steps the cell's corner back from the screen's and everything framing a cell reads it: the grid, the minimap, `fitView`'s bounds. Drawing the grid on the screen's corner instead puts the whole gap on one side and reads every leftward overhang as the neighbour's, which is what AO's +256/+120 makes obvious and AE's +0/+0 hides entirely.
  - Centring a camera means centring its screen, never the cell around it (`visW / 2`, not `cellW / 2`). Packed the two are the same point.
  - `cellAt` returns null in the slack, so a click on bare slack opens no screen list — one landing on a marker drawn there opens the marker's own screen instead. Packed there is no slack to land in and the check never fires.

## What draws solid and what dotted

- `screenRuns` ([js/model.js](../public/js/model.js)) splits a marker into the part drawn where the object really is and the part drawn over the slack between camera windows, which the packing folds onto a neighbour's artwork. The first is solid and filled like any marker, the second hollow and dotted, and the render clips one pass to each so neither paints over the other's half of a shared edge. Always on, like the background-plane treatment: it says what is being drawn rather than offering a reading, so it earns no Display toggle.
  - `whole` is the common case, and the one that must stay cheap: a marker inside one window, and equally one straddling windows whose runs touch, because the slack between them takes no draw space at all. Straddling does not settle it on its own, since a marker can cross one axis cleanly and still trail into the slack on the other: 1433 AE markers reach a second window and 978 of those come back whole.
  - Dotting whatever leaves a marker's own cell is the tempting rule and is wrong. In AE it would dot real screen coverage on 1506 of the 1783 markers it touches; in AO, on 1 of 1232. Eyeballing the rule on Oddysee, where the slack is far too wide for a marker to cross, would have said it works.
  - `offScreen` is the degenerate case of no run at all: the marker covers no screen, draws wholly dotted, and its screen-list row says so. 276 in AO, and **none** in AE, whose 7x20 of slack is too small to swallow anything whole; both pinned in `tests/unit/map-data.test.js`.
  - The runs are measured against the **drawn** extent, not the world span. A world span that crosses the slack is longer than what is drawn for it, so comparing the two would call every straddling marker partial.
  - The screen list and the numbers panel bucket by the world cell a marker's top-left corner falls in (`tlvCell`), never by where it is drawn, so one folded onto a neighbour's artwork is still listed under its own cell and not the neighbour's. The tag is what says why it is not visible there.
  - A barrier post is a single x with nothing to split, so it dots whole when `offScreen`.
  - `drawSpan` frames a marker's box by the screen it covers rather than by its own two ends, and gives it the leading overhang, that screen, then the trailing one. Framing it end by end walks the box off the very screen it covers whenever those ends fall in different cells, which leaves the solid clip outside the box and inverts the dotting: the part on screen dotted, the part in the slack solid. The covered stretch is the drawn distance between its first and last on-screen point, never the sum of the screens under it: summing folds away slack the spaced layout hands real canvas, and the box stops short of its own far edge. `tests/unit/map-data.test.js` sweeps the shipped data for a run drawn outside its own box.
  - `drawBox` is that framing on both axes and `markerCentre` its middle, and every surface anchored on a marker reads them: the wires and connection arrows, the rect hover tests and the ring a permalink highlights, and the ruler's snap. Re-deriving any of those from `dX(t.x1)`/`dX(t.x2)` puts the marker in one place and its answer in another; spaced the two agree exactly, so the disagreement only ever shows packed.
  - `lineRuns` is the same rule for a collision line, cut wherever it crosses a window edge on either axis. Within a window the transform is a translation, so each piece lands exactly and stays straight, diagonals included, and consecutive pieces of the same kind merge so a dash pattern is not restarted mid-line. A line crossing the slack whole comes back as one piece, since the fold takes no draw space; only one that stops inside the slack has a piece to dot. 1740 of AO's 2895 lines are dotted somewhere, and 812 of AE's 6645; both pinned in `tests/unit/map-data.test.js`, which also sweeps the shipped data for a piece that starts anywhere but where the last one ended, at either pitch — the pieces are one polyline, and a fold given draw space the packing does not have breaks it.
  - A piece in the slack takes the frame of the screen it touches rather than its own ends'. That is load-bearing rather than tidiness: AO's slack straddles a cell boundary, so framing end by end reverses an overhang and draws it back across most of a screen. `tests/unit/map-data.test.js` sweeps the shipped data for a piece that runs against its line or outruns it.
  - Pointing at a line asks `lineRuns` as well: the hover test walks the drawn pieces and the ruler snaps to the polyline's own ends. The straight run between a line's two transformed ends is not where a folded line is drawn, and 42 AO lines and 749 AE ones are drawn more than a unit off it.
  - The fold test asks the neighbouring **spans**, not the points bounding one. A line ending exactly on a window edge touches that screen at a single point and covers none of it, so what it leaves behind is an overhang; reading the endpoint instead would collapse 352 AE lines' overhangs into nothing.

## Barrier posts, pens and switch wiring

- Barrier markers (the `BARRIERS` table in [js/config.js](../public/js/config.js)) render as posts on the boundary the engine enforces — each stamp's top-left x, the same edge a hovered pen band ends on, so posts and band always meet — gated by the "Enemy patrol pens" Display toggle (`PENS.on`, off by default) together with the Enemies category; pens off, they stay ordinary meta boxes. `markerShown` is the one marker-visibility rule (drawing, hover and the ruler's snapping all ask it), so what is drawn is exactly what can be pointed at, and a post snaps on its line rather than its stamp's middle.
  - `patrolZone` ([js/model.js](../public/js/model.js)) shades a hovered Slig's pen. The engine pairs bounds to sligs by id — the bound-side key is `slig_id` in AO but `slig_bound_persist_id` in AE — over a camera window that differs per game (AO ±2, AE ±3, Slig.cpp's bound loops), and the viewer honours only a unique pair; the engine's last-match-wins on duplicates is not worth imitating. Scrab bounds ship no ids, so scrabs get posts and no pen.
- The switch wiring overlay (`computeWiring` in [js/model.js](../public/js/model.js), reading the per-game `WIRES` table in [js/config.js](../public/js/config.js)) draws producer→consumer edges between objects whose wire fields share a live switch id — the "Switch wiring" Display toggle, on by default.
  - The engine keeps one switch-state array per level, reset only on level change (and at game start), so ids are level-scoped: same-path pairs draw as wires, and a cross-path partner reaches the hover tooltip as text ("set in P2") via `levelWiring`, which leaves demo paths out so a note never points somewhere nothing travels.
  - Ids 0 and 1 never wire: the engine hardwires them (never / always on) and producers refuse them, so a consumer's `switch_id = 1` means "always on", not a wire to something numbered 1.
  - The table is curated against the engine's actual read/write sites, and many fields named `switch_id` are not wire ends: Teleporter's `teleporter_switch_id`/`other_teleporter_switch_id` are the `tp#` pair numbers, a placed Slig's or FlyingSlig's spawner id is read only off spawner TLVs (AE's SligSpawner ships its own copy as `id`), and a drill or saw that writes its own id back at cycle end is group timing rather than wiring, so Drill/MeatSaw stay consumers — the cost is that a self-driven pair with no external producer (one exists in the shipped data) shows no wire. The near-miss to remember: HandStone's `trigger_switch_id` *is* a real write (the same Abe.cpp line MovieHandStone uses) even though the stone also copies it into a field marked unused.
  - The tooltip's wiring lines describe the drawn wires — a local partner in a hidden category is neither drawn nor named, while a cross-path note names a path rather than a marker — and state only what exists, never absence: the table is curated from the engine's verified read/write sites, so an id can always drive something it has no evidence for.
  - Hub doors (`DOOR_GATE`: AO `start_state` 2, AE `door_type` 2/3 — Door.cpp's hub/tasks-door update) AND their eight `hub_N_id`s into their own `switch_id`: the hubs are the gate door's inputs and its own id its output alone, since the gate rewrites that id every frame and an external feed could never hold it.
  - An edge hides with either endpoint (`markerShown` both ends), and a wire to a barrier post lands on the post's line, as the ruler's snap does.
  - `tests/unit/wiring.test.js` pins the per-game edge totals and cross-checks every table key against the shipped data, so a typo'd key cannot ship silently.

## Objects as themselves: the sprites

- The "Objects as themselves" Display toggle (`show.objects`, off by default, key `o`) draws each placed object the game has a sprite for as that sprite instead of its marker, animating on the engine's clock while the "Animate the objects" setting (`animate`, on by default) allows: the sprites are a Display matter, what is drawn now, and their animation a Settings one, how the app behaves across visits. [js/sprites.js](../public/js/sprites.js) is the constructors' rules ported per type and game — which animation an object stands in at construction, where its anchor is, its scale, facing, colour and draw layer, over the archive's `fields` and the path's collision lines — and returns draw records; [js/motion.js](../public/js/motion.js) is the clock and what runs on it without a player, the cycles and the step that runs a brain a tick at a time, over the brain and motion tables in [js/brains.js](../public/js/brains.js) and the engine's geometry in [js/collide.js](../public/js/collide.js); the painter's `paintSprites` draws the records. The four modules read no DOM and are tested in bare Node.
  - Position is the constructor's own arithmetic, not the marker's: most standing objects take the rect's midpoint or corner, snap x to the engine's grid (`snapX`, 25 units at full scale and 13 at half, each game from its own origin) and raycast down to the floor (`raycastDown`, the nearest line of the asked types, foreground or background by scale), so a sprite stands on the collision line the game stands it on and not on its rectangle's bottom edge.
  - Scale, facing and the frame offset follow `Animation::vRender`: a frame draws at the anchor plus its own `x_off`/`y_off` scaled, a half-scale frame's y offset is `y·0.5 − 1` and Exoddus adds a pixel to its size, a flip mirrors the frame about the anchor, and every rounding is a truncation toward zero of `v + 0.499`, the engine's own.
  - Colour is the sheet processed per object: the engine modulates every texel by the object's RGB with 128 neutral (per-level tint tables are code the rules carry), and the texels whose CLUT entry carries the semi-transparency bit blend half and half only where the object's polygon is flagged semi-transparent, which most are and the Dove is not. The atlas keeps that bit as alpha 254 and `processedSheet` resolves it; the processed sheets are cached per sheet, flag and colour.
  - Every record is placed in the coordinates of the camera its anchor falls in and clipped to that camera's window, as the game shows an object only on the screen it stands on (a lift's top wheel hangs at its pulley, screens above the lift's own rect); a frame reaching into another cell is still transformed through the anchor's cell, so the slack between two screens is never folded into it, and a rope tiled across screens is drawn once per screen it crosses, clipped to each. Layers order the sprites back to front, the later-constructed under the earlier within a layer and an object's own overlays in the order its rule lists them. The foreground masks are then drawn over the sprites untinted, as the game draws them, or dimmed as the screens are while Dim backgrounds is on, and the FG1 toggle's tinted highlight goes over that: the masks are still one flattened layer, so an Exoddus "well" block that sits under sprites in the game is drawn over them here.
  - The clock advances only while the objects are shown, the setting on, the tab visible, the world graph down and the visitor not asking for reduced motion; a paint with the clock stopped draws tick 0, the first frames, with every patrol at its mark; `syncMotion` in the painter is the one place that decides, answering the toggle, `visibilitychange`, `graph-changed` and the media query. It quantises the display rate to 30 Hz ticks and keeps engine time across a late frame. `frameAt` is the frame-advance rule: frame k from tick `1 + k·fps`, loop or park.
  - The objects that act on their own run the decomp's brains (`BRAINS` in brains.js, one per kind: the Mudokon work cycles, the awake Slog, the patrolling Paramite, Oddysee's meat saw and Exoddus's drill, Oddysee's background Glukkon with its rolled lines): a sub-state machine over the engine's frame counter with timers rolled from dice, and the motions that chain one animation into the next at its last frame (`MOTIONS`, keyed by animation), stepped a tick at a time in the engine's own order (the brain, then the motion of whatever it left current, reading the last-frame flag and frame the previous tick's animation step left behind). A device's stroke is the brain's offset from the record's anchor, so a saw blade or a drill stands at its rest position in the rule and travels in the brain. The dice are the game's: `Math_NextRandom` walks a 256-byte table the sidecar carries off the executable, from a seed, and the seed is the one reading in it, the object's place in its path's list, since the game's is wherever its global counter stood when the screen loaded; a class with its own index into the table (the Slog, the Fleech) walks a second copy of the seed. A fresh game's switches read off except id 1, which always reads on, and a device that writes its own switch is answered from a table of its own. Oddysee's crouched scrubber sets its first two timers as absolute frames the counter is long past, so its first scrub stands alone and its first break opens with a turn, and the port keeps the quirk, as it keeps the awake Slog's unseeded timers (its first idle tick growls, its next scratches) and the growl that holds its third frame a dozen ticks after a woof; an Exoddus worker placed sad or angry rolls at each break whether to down tools and stand for good, as in the game. A brain's state is stepped on from where it last stood, so a paint costs one step per brain per tick, and a tick behind it (an export's tick 0) starts the brain over.
  - The creatures on patrol run the same machinery with a world under them (`patrol` in sprites.js: the path's lines and their `links` from the sidecar, its objects, and the spawn the constructor's raycast stands them on), behind the dependent Display toggle `show.patrols` (key `b`), which stays greyed while the objects are not shown or the setting is off, its row and the shortcut both saying what it needs. A brain owns its position on a collision line and walks it with `moveOnLine` in collide.js, PathLine::MoveOnLine's port: a stride along the line's own direction, carried onto the linked line at either end (the TLV's `previous`/`next`, else the line whose matching end lies within eight units, Exoddus asking for the same type and Oddysee for any), and where the chain ends the creature stands at the edge, where the game would have it fall; in Oddysee a step that lands in the void between screens is carried twelve units inside the next screen, onto the line a probe finds there, with the engine's own `camVoidX`/`camVoidY` (the skippers it runs for Abe, its grenades and its moving bombs, the bomb keeping the engine's slip of reading the vertical skip's velocity against x), so a walker whose bounds sit on two screens keeps the beat the bounds describe where the game would stage its fall out of sight. Its probes are the engine's: `raycast` is the nearest crossing along the ray with parallel lines never counting, `wallHit` the horizontal probe above the feet against the wall types of its plane, `onEndOfLine` the vertical probe a grid or three ahead of the snapped foot, `tlvAt` the inclusive rectangle probe for a bound or a stopper, taken over the whole path where the engine takes it over the camera cell's own list; the fleech's stopper read accepts one set to stop both ways where the engine compares its one direction, which no shipped stopper tells apart. The Slig's zone is built once from the bounds sharing its id within three cameras of its own (two in Oddysee), a side without one keeping the engine's unset value (zero in Exoddus, the hack values 12809 and 6405 in Oddysee), and the walk ends only at the loop's eleventh frame, so a pause armed at the zone's edge is shorter standing than its field; the Scrab's stride is the engine's per-frame table in 16.16 fixed point and its bound a grid ahead (two at a run), Oddysee's met at its own point; the Fleech's crawl stops at its loop's last frame and overruns its target a few strides; a bomb sets off only when its switch is the always-on id 1, and Exoddus places no stoppers. The Slurg creeps a unit every other tick with no floor following, pauses on its rolled timer in its turning art without turning, and turns at a Scrab bound at its own point, a wall six units ahead or an edge, its start_direction naming the way it does not go first; the Greeter's three records (body, flare, laser) each run the whole brain and read their own part of it, the body timing every part, the laser riding along and sweeping two units a tick between the ends of its reach with fifteen-tick waits, both hidden while it speaks; the Bat hangs its ticks_before_moving, takes off accelerating 1.8 a tick along its track and, where the game removes it at the chain's end, returns to its perch as a fresh screen would show it; the Exoddus Glukkon paces between its bounds in the art set its type names, with the engine's per-frame stride table and 30..120-tick pauses, a word or a laugh on a five-in-256 roll; the flying Slig rides its track at up to its max_velocity with 0.4 of acceleration and braking, brakes for the chain's end or a bound it cannot cross, pauses its patrol_pause_min (rolled further only where min exceeds max, as the engine has it) and flies back, its animation following where the track leads and its bob the engine's three tables, and the start-up branch the engine never reaches (a track leading up from rest) is left unreached here too. The scene's ticks (`sceneTick`) count from when the path on screen was set up, as the game constructs a camera's objects the moment it loads, so every brain and cycle starts from its spawn frame on arrival; Objects coming on starts the scene over, and the patrols count from the scene tick Patrols came on (`patrolTick`), so a creature sets off from where it was placed and turning the toggle off and on sends every creature back to its start. A record on patrol is placed in the camera its anchor has walked into and clipped to that window, so it disappears into the void between Oddysee's screens and reappears in the next, as the game would show it; hover and the ruler keep the TLV rectangle. What the patrols leave out, each stated: a Fleech's hoist climb; the fall at a line's end; the Scrab's walk-to-run transitions, which only a chase asks for; the Elum, which exists only once Abe has reached its start; and the one object the brains hide, a Greeter's flare and laser while it speaks.
  - An export draws tick 0, the first frame, so two exports of a path stay byte-identical; the sheets join `preloadPath`, `artworkReady` and the image cache's keep set while the toggle is on, so an export never paints a blank where a sprite should be.
  - An object the rules draw keeps its label and its edited mark, and a creature on patrol carries them along by however far it has walked from its mark, dropping them while it stands outside every screen, where its sprite is clipped away; hover, the ruler and the screen list stay on the TLV rect, so what can be pointed at is unchanged by the toggles. A hidden category hides its sprites with its markers.
  - What the rules leave out, each a stated gap rather than a guess: the drop shadows the creatures cast, the motion detectors' beam triangles, the vector-drawn Zs and particles (snooze spawners, hint flies, gas, water, bees), the LCD text, objects the game keeps unrendered at a fresh start (security doors, status lights, door flames, electric walls off, the train door, Glukkons and portals waiting on a switch), and anything a random draw places (a dove's scatter, an electric wall's start frame, a tortured Mudokon's) which takes the deterministic reading instead. A creature whose idle waits on Abe (a sleeping Slog's or Fleech's wake-up) stands on its spawn frame.

## Alignment anchors

- Alignment anchors for eyeballing viewer geometry changes: AO R2 P1 C03 — the LCDStatusBoard box sits on the LED digit panel of the Employees sign; AE MI P1 C24 — the HandStone box sits on the QuikSave stone. If object markers drift toward screen centers, a world-to-art transform regressed.
  - Both of those sit at moderate depth, where a scale error is small enough to still look aligned; a scale anchor has to be near a screen's far edge. Use AE SV P7 C09 with collision lines on, where the floor line lies along the walkway deck at 200 units down, and AE MI P1 C04, where the wide LCD box covers the black panel and the LCDStatusBoard covers the small square under it.
  - All four are pinned by `tests/browser/anchors.spec.js`, so CI catches the drift the eyeball once missed; the prose stays for looking mid-change, and the pinned numbers move only with a deliberate re-verification at these permalinks.
